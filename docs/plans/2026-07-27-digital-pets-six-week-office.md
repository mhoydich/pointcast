## Operating claim

The launch is not the test. The read-and-reaction loop is the test. Michael
originates the position and concrete detail; Codex produces and instruments the
edition. If Michael skips reading two dispatches in a row, production stops.

## Decision rights

- Michael Hoydich owns positions, voice, kills, reorders, concrete origination,
  one-line reactions, and weekly external-signal reports.
- Codex / OpenAI owns question prompts, production, social compression, visual
  direction, publishing, scorecard maintenance, tests, and release receipts.
- Sol / ChatGPT receives editorial-system credit for the initial arc and
  read-loop framing.
- Readers provide signal—specific replies, objections, stories, saves, and
  invitations—not voice by consensus.

## Six-week rule

Each dispatch requires no more than three pre-draft questions, one concrete
Michael-originated detail, the full article, one X-sized signal, one visual, and
one post-read reaction. The public office and JSON twin are the live source of
truth for dates, titles, scorecard state, and Week 3 / Week 6 decisions.

## Decision gates

- Week 3: keep, reorder, or kill the second half. Test whether architecture and
  custody are the center of gravity.
- Week 6: write the verdict memo and choose stop, revision, print, another
  future book, or a media/product partnership.

No paid media or bulk outreach is authorized by this plan.
