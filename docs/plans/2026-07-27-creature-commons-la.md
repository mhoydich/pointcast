## Chef’s-choice decisions

- Adults first: makers, collectors, repairers, and curious households.
- Paired form: a public commons beside an authored creature studio.
- Ninety-day proof: three non-sale reference creatures, a Memory Passport, a
  Local Life Standard, and recurring build and repair sessions.

## Operating claim

A company can publish a creature. Only an institution can keep the conditions
of care from disappearing with the company.

The commons stewards shared continuity infrastructure: offline-life and memory
standards, repair and parts knowledge, supplier learning, workshops, and the
archive/graveyard protocol. The studio retains named creatures, canon,
editions, performances, and commercial collaborations.

## Pilot sequence

1. Days 1–14: recruit a five-to-seven-person founding circle and write five
   one-page promises covering local life, memory, repair, privacy, and ending.
2. Days 15–45: meet one electronics assembler, one soft-goods developer, one
   enclosure shop, and one compliance advisor; build three non-sale references.
3. Days 46–75: run one closed build circle and one repair night; publish the
   Memory Passport and Local Life Standard as discussion drafts.
4. Days 76–90: present the opened bodies in confirmed partner space, publish
   the field guide, and decide whether any entity is warranted.

## Boundaries

- No incorporation or charitable-status claim.
- No fundraising campaign or charitable donations.
- No facility, lease, or public-space activation.
- No vendor commitment, quote, endorsement, or claimed product fit.
- No public product sale.
- No children’s product or study.
- No legal, tax, compliance, or safety assurance.

## Decision after ninety days

Choose one:

- continue as an informal publishing and build project;
- apply for fiscal sponsorship around a proven educational/public program;
- form a cooperative if member economic ownership is the actual need;
- maintain a taxable authored studio with a separate commons partner;
- stop because the institution did not become real in practice.

The organization is not the output. Better promises, repairable reference
bodies, and public evidence are the output.
