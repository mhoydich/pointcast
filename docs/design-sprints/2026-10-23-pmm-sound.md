## 0. Where sound is today

`blip(up)` in `public/pmm/app.js`, around line 122, is the whole system. It plays two square-wave notes, 440 then 660 Hz or the reverse, at gain 0.05, straight to `ac.destination`. It is gated on `state.me.sound` and on a first gesture. `buzz(ms)` at line 57 makes one fixed vibration.

There are 11 call sites. They use the same two sounds for a sent message, a received message, a connection, a reaction, a mood change and a failed send. Nothing depends on mood. Nothing passes through a compressor or limiter. A burst of 20 messages plays 20 blips.

## 2. Three directions

All three are playable in the lab, section 1. Each covers send, receive, connect, fail, react and their-mood, uses the shared durations, and stays within the same gain budget. They live in `sketches.js`.

**SID.** Pulse-width square leads. Chords are played as 30 ms arpeggios, the trick one-voice-per-channel chips use to fake a chord. Percussion is noise. Receive arpeggiates the sender's chord downward in a 12.5% pulse. Connect is three 4-note chord stabs, then their arpeggio climbing two octaves.
*Heard:* the most delightful and the most "PointCast". By the fortieth message it is tiring, though. The arpeggios reach 3 kHz in square waves, which lands right in the band the ear is most sensitive to.

**VCS.** Every pitch is snapped to a 32-step clock-divider set, the constraint of a 2600-class chip. Most notes land a little off the piano: A4 becomes 436 Hz. Tones are a one-third pulse with a narrow noise band an octave up. Fail is five stepped notes falling through the divider set.
*Heard:* characterful and funny once, then grating. Because the detuning is everywhere, a wrong sound can't sound any more wrong than a right one.

**Glass.** Sine and triangle only, slow attacks, low gain. Send is one sine glide. Receive is a soft fifth. Their-mood plays both motifs in sine and triangle.
*Heard:* lovely in a quiet room and lost on a phone speaker in a café. Send and receive are easy to confuse, and nothing says "PointCast".

## 3. Decide

Each criterion is scored 1–5, where 5 is best. The fatigue score answers the question "is it still welcome after 200 messages?". The loudness-safety score looks at spectral content in the 2–5 kHz band and at peak behaviour, not just RMS. The lab's 200-message demo plays any of the four systems for comparison.

| | delight | distinct earcons | fatigue at 200 | loudness safety | brand fit | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SID | 5 | 4 | 1 | 2 | 5 | 17 |
| VCS | 3 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 15 |
| Glass | 2 | 2 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 16 |
| **Chosen: SID bones, Glass manners** | 4 | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | **23** |

**Decision:** combine them, split by how often each cue fires.

- **Rare moments that matter** (boot, connect, settle, file-done, mood) get SID: pulse-wave arpeggios with the mood's chord.
- **Cues that repeat all day** (send, receive, react, tab, leave) get Glass manners: short, filtered, mostly triangle and sine. Square-wave moods keep their pulse on receive but pass through a tight low-pass.
- **Things that went wrong** (fail, unverified) get VCS: off-grid, detuned pitches, used *only* here, so detuning itself means "wrong". These two never change with mood, so they always sound the same.

Every receive is the opening two notes of the sender's Mood Atlas motif. After a day you can hear who is calm and who is salty before you look.

## 4. The system

**Synth** (`scheduleNotes`):

- **Voices.** Square with any pulse width (a 40-harmonic PeriodicWave, loudness-compensated by 0.5/√(pw(1−pw)), so a narrow pulse isn't quieter), triangle, sine, and seeded noise through a band-pass.
- **Envelope.** Linear ADSR whose release reaches zero exactly at `t + dur`, so cues never click and end when the table says.
- **Arpeggios and glides.** `arp()` builds a note per interval; any note can glide (`to`).
- **Per-voice low-pass.** Every square voice has one at 3–5× its pitch.
- **Master chain.** High shelf at −6 dB above 2.8 kHz → DynamicsCompressor (−14 dB threshold, 5:1) → volume.

**Engine behaviour:**

- **Gesture gate.** Nothing sounds before `unlock()`, which runs inside a gesture and plays one silent sample for iOS.
- **Folding bursts.** A cue repeating inside its own length plus 160 ms folds into the first. Twenty messages at once make one knock and one buzz.
- **Fading streaks.** Each repeat of a cue inside six seconds plays 5% quieter, down to 55%.
- **Voice cap.** At most 14 voices. A new cue that won't fit is dropped, except connect, fail, hangup and unverified, which clear the board.
- **Hidden tab.** Silent while `document.hidden`, unless the event is a receive and the user opted in.
- **Atmosphere bed.** Off by default. A 9.6 s loop: their motif slowed and an octave down in sine, yours two octaves down in triangle, with 0.5 s swells and a 0.12 peak. It is the only thing allowed to sustain past one second.

**Mood flavour.** A mood's root is the first note of its motif, in the motif's own register. Its timbre is the motif's wave: triangle for calm, tired and low; square for hyped, sunny, salty and curious; sine for soft. Each mood also has a chord shape (major, minor, or a Lydian question for curious) and a pulse width (hyped 25%, sunny 37.5%, salty 12.5%, curious 30%). The same earcon is transposed and re-timbred by mood. Receive takes the sender's mood. Every other cue takes yours, except fail and unverified, which take none.

**1. Imports.**

- Delete `public/pmm/sound/moods-temp.js`.
- Change the one import in `sound.js` to `../moods/moods.js`.
- Point the lab's import at the same file.

**2. Load lazily from the classic script.** app.js is an IIFE, not a module, so it uses dynamic `import()`. Importing creates no AudioContext. Start the import at idle, not on the gesture, so that `unlock()` can run *synchronously* inside the first gesture, which iOS requires:

```js
let snd = null;
const loadSound = () => import('./sound/sound.js').then((m) => { snd = m; m.setEnabled(state.me.sound !== false); m.setHaptics(state.me.haptics !== false); m.setHiddenReceive(Boolean(state.me.bgsound)); return m; }, () => null);
(window.requestIdleCallback || setTimeout)(loadSound);
const cue = (event, opts) => { if (gestured && snd) snd.play(event, { mood: state.me.mood, ...opts }); };
```

**3. The gesture listener at line 55.** It currently sets `gestured` once. Change it to also call `snd?.unlock()` on every `pointerdown` or `keydown` until `snd.stats().state === 'running'`. iOS can suspend the context again after a call or Siri, and the next tap revives it. On the first successful unlock, `cue('boot')` once per page load.

**5. Settings.**

- **SND maps one to one.** `state.me.sound` (default true) → `snd.setEnabled()`, in the settings save handler at line 196 and at load. Switching sound off also stops the bed.
- **Index.html line 172 label.** "SOUND: square-wave blips" becomes "SOUND: chip-tune cues".
- **Three new settings**, all persisted in `state.me`:
  - `haptics`, default on, mapped to `setHaptics`;
  - `atmo`, the atmosphere bed, default **off**;
