## Outcome

The current local-draft inventory passes the scoped rights/provenance checks: **18 hosted licensed/public-domain photographs, 3 expressly labeled original AI-generated artworks, and 6 outbound-only references**. Each hosted photograph has an explicit license or photographer’s public-domain release, a creator credit, a source page, a caption, and a date. No audio/video files, playback embeds, or autoplay markup were found in the exhibition scope.

The parent repaired the two incorrect generic Flickr source links and the Paris source-crop credit during this review; the repaired values were read back from the staged JSON. No further photo removal is recommended. The previously excluded Lovesexy vest display must remain outbound-only because of the separately displayed portrait. The 1984 publicity portrait must remain outbound-only because its Commons public-domain rationale is U.S.-specific.

Its current `1979-01-05` value describes the historical object, not the capture date of the modern museum image. The existing caption already identifies it as the museum’s image of the ticket.

## Underlying works, source crops, and ShareAlike

The independent visual review inspected the downloaded contact sheet and all three generated full-resolution artworks. It found no imported restricted album cover, film still, framed portrait, or scanned editorial photograph embedded within any hosted photograph. The jacket is a garment study rather than a photograph of separate displayed art. The ticket’s name, event details, prices, ordinary lettering, and border do not introduce a substantial separate pictorial work. The First Avenue close-ups show simple stars and band names. These are observations about the downloaded compositions, not an assertion that a photographer owns every object in view.

The [Copyright Office’s useful-article guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/register/va-useful.html) distinguishes garment function from separable pictorial/graphic/sculptural features. Its [Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf) explains the exclusions for names, short phrases, lettering, and familiar shapes. The exterior venue/studio photographs depict publicly visible U.S. buildings; [17 U.S.C. §120(a)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#120) permits pictorial representations of such constructed architecture. No photograph is represented as a license to manufacture a costume, use a trademark as branding, or obtain endorsement.

The Paris item is a **licensed still extracted from the creator’s video**, not an official concert-release frame. Its Commons license review and fresh creator check support the retained still; its image contains a performer, instruments, and an LED-grid background, without visible album art, film projection, lyrics, or notation. This review does **not** treat the source’s CC label as permission to rehost its audio, the full concert video, the musical composition, or other audiovisual material. None is hosted here. The current YouTube HTML did not expose the license field, so the license basis is the documented Commons review of the source, rather than an invented current direct-license observation.

The exhibition credits source creators and links each hosted photo’s license in both the gallery and lightbox. BY-SA photos remain individually identified under their chosen license. No editorial recoloring/retouching or new raster crop was applied to these photos; technical thumbnail sizing is documented. The preexisting Paris crop is now expressly identified. If a future edit creates a photo derivative, it must retain the corresponding ShareAlike license and change notice. The [BY-SA 2.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/legalcode.en) distinguishes collecting unmodified works from adapting them; placing individually licensed photographs alongside original prose/art does not make the whole editorial page a BY-SA photograph derivative.

## Generated artwork and media/text

All three original-art PNGs were visually inspected: **Rain / Light**, **Violet / Material**, and **Electric / Crescendo**. They are visibly contemporary illustrations, with no identifiable Prince likeness, official logo, album-cover recreation, fake documentary photograph, or readable lyrics. The fashion asset is an invented material/silhouette study. The stage asset is an abstract guitar/crowd/light composition that does not claim to depict an identifiable historical concert.

The original-art provenance records tool, prompt, creation date, absence of supplied third-party reference images, and absence of endorsement. The staged JSON retains prompts and generated-art status without host filesystem paths. Gallery captions explicitly identify AI generation/contemporary interpretation; the hero also identifies original AI-generated artwork. The JSON correctly does not call these assets PD or CC. Original PNGs are preserved and the WebP delivery transformations are recorded.

The Astro/TypeScript scope contains no lyric reproductions, copied full articles, `<audio>`, `<video>`, `<iframe>`, or autoplay. Listening is implemented as user-initiated outbound official-provider links. The hosted directory contains JPG/PNG/WebP assets only. The review found original explanatory prose, short work/category titles, and source links rather than a reproduced song or article. Fact accuracy is being reviewed separately.
