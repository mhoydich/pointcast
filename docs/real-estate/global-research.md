# Global property study: research notes and conventions

Research checked **2026-10-03**. The accompanying module is [`src/lib/real-estate-global.mjs`](../../src/lib/real-estate-global.mjs). Its eight market cards are a curated educational starting point. They do not establish property availability, a buyer’s eligibility, current loan terms, or investment merit. Opportunity theses and strategy questions are editorial inferences; the cited authorities support the rules and definitions described alongside them.

The selected markets deliberately expose different ownership, tenancy, tax, currency and operating systems: Los Angeles, London, Tokyo, Singapore, Dubai, Berlin, Mexico City and São Paulo. They are not ranked by yield. A city-wide average would also conceal the difference between an occupied condominium, an uncompleted development, a leased warehouse and a listed company share. No observed asking price, transaction price or market yield is inserted into these comparison cards.

## How to use the comparison

Start with the legal interest and operating mechanism. A building title, a leasehold interest, a share in a property company and a claim on a mortgage produce different rights and different cash flows. Then identify the property and buyer profile, verify lawful use and leases, build the local-currency operating statement, obtain actual finance terms, and model the eventual exit. Convert to home currency only after naming the exchange-rate direction and costs.

For each market, the module separates `access`, `thesis`, `risks`, `tax`, `financing` and `operating`. Access describes a verified rule or a diligence requirement; it is not a legal eligibility decision. Financing is deliberately a question for documented loan terms. The source list has an access-check date and a cadence description. **Checked today** does not mean **effective today**, and it does not mean a dataset is real time.

The regional comparison is useful for asking better questions. It cannot substitute for local conveyancing, tax, building, insurance or lending evidence. The individual buyer’s nationality, residency, entity, other holdings and tax treaties are unknown. No personal budget, ownership group commitments or financing ability have been assumed.

## Cash-flow conventions

The module’s definitions are educational conventions, stated so the arithmetic can be checked. They are not substitutes for a lender’s underwriting worksheet or a tax return.

- Scheduled rent is the rent before vacancy and credit loss. Effective gross income subtracts that loss and adds separately identified other income.
- Operating NOI here deducts recurring property operating costs, including the stated repair, management, property-tax and insurance allowances. It precedes debt service, income tax, depreciation, capex and replacement reserves.
- After-reserve income deducts replacement-reserve funding. Pre-tax cash to equity then deducts debt service and any separately modeled, unfunded capital expenditure.
- An opening reserve and subsequent reserve transfers remain cash assets until spent. They reduce distributable cash. A model must avoid counting the same roof once as reserve funding and again as an expense without releasing funded reserve cash.
- Acquisition costs and initial works are included in total acquisition cost. Opening cash reserves are additionally included in initially committed equity. A sale deducts exit costs, remaining debt and applicable tax/settlement amounts before producing liquid proceeds.

The [OCC March 2022 CRE handbook](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/commercial-real-estate-lending/pub-ch-commercial-real-estate.pdf), notably printed pages 42–44 and its glossary, describes underwriting, stress testing, capitalization and debt coverage. Its lender NOI convention can include imputed replacement reserves. This module therefore labels **operating DSCR** and **after-reserve DSCR** separately instead of silently combining conventions. Required lender thresholds also vary; a model threshold does not establish approval.

[CFPB’s Closing Disclosure explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/closing-disclosure/) helps distinguish principal and interest, tax/insurance escrow, loan costs, down payment and cash to close. [Fannie Mae’s rental-income guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.1-08/rental-income) further shows why projected rent and qualifying borrower income are different questions. That entry was dated 2 September 2026 when checked; a model does not implement all its loan-program conditions.

## A denominator lesson, using invented numbers

The fixed example is a hypothetical USD rental. It is not a Los Angeles listing or a suggested budget. Assumptions: $500,000 price, $25,000 acquisition costs, $10,000 initial works, $36,000 scheduled annual rent, 5% vacancy/credit loss, $12,500 recurring operating costs, $3,000 annual replacement-reserve funding, $300,000 debt, $18,000 annual debt service and $12,000 opening cash reserve.

| Calculation | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Vacancy/credit loss: $36,000 × 5% | $1,800 |
| Effective gross income: $36,000 − $1,800 | $34,200 |
| Operating NOI: $34,200 − $12,500 | $21,700 |
| After-reserve income: $21,700 − $3,000 | $18,700 |
| Cash after reserves and debt: $18,700 − $18,000 | $700 |
| Total acquisition cost: $500,000 + $25,000 + $10,000 | $535,000 |
| Initially committed equity: $535,000 − $300,000 + $12,000 | $247,000 |
| Gross yield on price | 7.20% |
| Operating NOI yield on total acquisition cost | 4.06% |
| Cap rate using operating NOI and price | 4.34% |
| Operating DSCR / after-reserve DSCR | 1.21× / 1.04× |
| Loan-to-price, with no appraisal supplied | 60.00% |
| Pre-income-tax cash-on-cash | 0.28% |

The example deliberately makes a 7.2% gross yield coexist with $700 of annual cash. The difference is explained by the waterfall and denominators, not by changing the rent. It omits personal taxes, tax benefits, extraordinary repairs, appreciation, refinancing, sale and FX. Debt service is an independent invented annual assumption; it is not reverse-engineered into a current loan offer.

## Ownership wrappers and workload categories

[SEC REIT guidance](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/investment-products/real-estate-investment-trusts-reits) separates public exchange-traded and non-traded structures and explains why liquidity, valuation, fees, conflicts and distribution funding need review. [IRS REIT instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120rei) describe the US qualification/distribution calculation; the 90% threshold is tied to a statutory taxable-income measure and is not a promised yield or 90% of rent. A mortgage REIT’s financing exposure differs from an equity REIT’s property exposure. Neither gives a shareholder individual registered title to a building.

`propertyTiers` describes capital commitment and work, from shared exposure through small direct ownership, multifamily operations, commercial leases, operating businesses and transformation. “Tier” is not a quality, wealth or return ranking. Each category can fail through an unsuitable basis, debt, rules or execution. `ownershipComparison` separately describes the rights and cash-flow wrapper so property type and security type are not conflated.

`strategies` supplies study prompts: income, useful improvement, adaptive reuse, longer commercial leases, public equity, credit and cross-border comparisons. These describe mechanisms and tradeoffs, not which strategy someone should pursue. Property demand, achievable rents, legal permissions and execution costs remain to be evidenced.

## Data access and freshness

This module is a sourced static content snapshot. It does not fetch live prices, legal updates or listings. Its `checkedAt` field is the date the sources were inspected. The separate site feed module and API are responsible for any implemented refresh behavior; this file makes no such promise.

Two discovery findings are relevant to the broader data inventory:

- [Dubai Land Department’s real-estate catalog](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/open-data/real-estate-data/) exposes public transaction/rental and related categories through date filters and download forms. A verified catalog is not a tested keyless API. Automated access, stable schema, CORS, rate limits and reuse licensing were not established here; no scraping or automated refresh is implemented by this module.
- [Banxico table CF102](https://www.banxico.org.mx/SieInternet/consultarDirectorioInternetAction.do?sector=6&accion=consultarCuadro&idCuadro=CF102&locale=en) is a banking-day exchange-rate reference. Its FIX methodology and publication timing do not make it a real-time executable currency quote. No feed integration is implemented here.

Official guidance can be linked without redistributing a proprietary listing database. No restricted MLS, listing photos, personal financial notes, credentials or private partner data were accessed or copied. No legal-source publisher is represented as a project partner. The educational project does not recruit students or claim that an intern program or institutional collaboration is active.
