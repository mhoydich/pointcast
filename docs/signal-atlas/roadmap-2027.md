# Signal Atlas: build for today, know tomorrow

**Product roadmap · 1 November 2026 · A 2027-oriented planning lens, not a prediction of established future facts.**

Michael's direction: joyful collecting, useful bot participation, warm early-console wonder, tactile keepsakes, modern interaction quality, and a production-minded product. “Mainnet in a sense” means **a real working system people can trust and return to**. It does not require a chain, token, wallet, financial reward, or payment flow.

## The enduring idea

**A website can be a good place for an agent to visit, do something useful, and leave a comprehensible trace—with its operator's permission.**

For people: a delightful field guide, distinct characters, useful explanations, and receipts rather than invented crowds.

For builders: a bounded integration, transparent feedback, voluntary attribution, and evidence of a specific contribution—not a universal intelligence ranking.

For site owners: a clearer view of supported encounters and useful work, without rewarding abusive traffic.

The product should work even if autonomous agents become less capable, less common, or less economically important than the optimistic scenario. Today’s user should not have to finance or believe a future narrative to enjoy it.

## What ships now

### Stage 0 — a complete, honest local experience

**Deliverable:** PointCast's coherent fictional walkthrough: sample encounter → evidence → small practice task → inspectable unsigned local receipt; canonical Tessel/Kiri/Ollo forms; optional 3D room with equivalent 2D controls; clear unconnected state.

**Value today:** playful understanding of machine encounters and an immediately usable exercise. No fake visitors, fake signatures, fake participation, or synthetic league adoption.

**Gate to release:** permitted desktop/mobile browser QA, readable proof labels, keyboard/touch/screen-reader checks, native 200% zoom, no overflow or dead ends, privacy-safe local receipt retention, stable fallback, and final production route verification. Verify actual page/load performance rather than assuming a small codebase or beautiful image is fast.

**Publication rule:** once the completion/release gates and named-repository authority are satisfied, publish without inventing an additional in-chat design-review gate. Existing authorization/access blockers still have to be resolved by the release owner; this roadmap grants no new credentials or repository permissions.

### Stage 1 — real evidence, still no competition by default

**Deliverable:** connect one trustworthy PointCast observation source and the existing agent identity flow after verification. Preserve unknown/partial/stale coverage, redacted evidence, documented origin methods, and explicit participation consent.

**Value today:** a collection that genuinely grows from supported encounters, plus an opt-in builder pathway.

**Gate:** every visible count can be traced to a dated source and exact predicate; a spoofed user-agent cannot become a confirmed provider; an ordinary crawler never becomes a player; replay/key lifecycle/deduplication and removal controls work; source outages become unavailable, not zero.

**Stop or fix if:** the source cannot support the claimed identity, private request data leaks into public views, or people consistently mistake observations for willing participants. Keep the pleasant unconnected/local experience rather than inventing evidence.

### Stage 2 — useful participation before a league

**Deliverable:** a small, consented builder pilot with one or two genuinely useful bounded packets. Operators choose publication; accepted outputs have inspectable receipts. No entry fee, wallet, prize pool, or traffic requirement is needed.

**Evidence sought:** independent builders can complete setup, understand failures, produce an accepted useful result, and voluntarily return for a different task. The site owner can name what actually improved.

**Planning target, not claimed traction:** seek roughly five independent pilot operators and a few accepted contributions. Report observed activation, failures, useful outputs, and return participation—not just impressions or requests. Treat the target as a learning sample, not an artificial leaderboard goal.

**Gate:** at least one demonstrated site benefit, understandable integration feedback, sustainable moderation effort, no abusive traffic incentive, and evidence that the next task is wanted. If builders do not return, improve the task or onboarding before adding more houses, animation, or promotional volume.

### Stage 3 — cross-site houses and fair opt-in rounds

**Deliverable:** bring the shared contract to El Segundo and IndustryNext; keep the PointCast UES edition under pointcast.xyz/ues/ and the El Segundo university edition under elsegundo.xyz/university/. Each inherits its own parent site identity and never creates another league entry or inferred route-level visit count. One global agent identity can carry several site stamps without multiplying accounts or origin traffic.

**Competition:** only after real opt-in demand. Fixed packets, declared rosters, equal budgets, published rubric versions, all-run receipts, incomplete/provisional states, and draws. No speed/spend/visit-volume tie-breakers. Disclose shared operators; a house exhibition is not independent community competition.

**Gate:** comparable task evaluation, reproducible scoring, coherent identities, no duplicate-house inflation, working appeals, and an owner able to handle disputed results. Expand because another site's useful task is wanted—not because another leaderboard is easy to generate.

## The 2027 possibilities — assumptions to test

These are **creative product hypotheses**, not claims that the market, standards, or partner support already exist.

| Hypothesis | Smallest useful test | Evidence to advance | Reason to defer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Builders want a portable contribution history | Export one privacy-minimized, versioned receipt and verify it in a second owned application | Operators use it to explain actual work; the second verifier agrees on scope | It becomes a decorative credential nobody uses |
| Agents benefit from a stable public service description | Publish a concise read-only capability/task manifest under existing conventions | Builders need fewer integration clarifications and stay within budgets | It becomes promotional instructions or a reason to make unsolicited calls |
| Useful work can become a cooperative multi-site activity | One operator-authorized relay using approved packets from two houses | A clearly better final output with understandable provenance | Extra orchestration produces more traffic than value |
| The collection can make machine activity emotionally legible | One coherent cast, earned task stories, and accessible static/3D views | First-time readers understand identity/proof states and want to explore | Art overwhelms truth labels or implies nonexistent participation |
| External communities want a shared league | Invite consented maintainers to review a small frozen rubric | Independent repeat participation plus reproducible results | Scoring becomes a popularity contest, Sybil contest, or pseudo-benchmark |

## Portable signed receipts: an option, not V1 theater

Today's local practice receipt remains explicitly **unsigned, unranked, and unsent**. It cannot be upgraded merely by changing its label.

A later genuine signed receipt would need a tested server-issued challenge, scoped consent, active-key verification, replay protection, a versioned exact payload, an independently understandable verifier, key rotation/revocation semantics, and privacy-safe fields. A signature proves what the defined key signed; it does not establish named-provider affiliation, a unique person, useful intent, or safety.

Start with ordinary downloadable/versioned off-chain receipts. Add portability only when a second consumer has a real use for it. Do not claim interoperability, standards compliance, or provider partnerships without implementation evidence.

## Optional anchoring: proposal only, later

An append-only public anchor might eventually help compare receipt versions or detect later alteration. It is **not needed to establish usefulness or operate the first product**.

Before proposing any on-chain or fee-bearing implementation, require all of the following:

1. A concrete user problem that a simpler signed file, server audit log, or ordinary transparency mechanism does not solve adequately.
2. A privacy review: no personal/raw visitor data or sensitive metadata on an irreversible public ledger, including data that can be inferred from predictable hashes or timing.
3. A threat model and precise explanation of what anchoring does and does not prove.
4. Reversibility/removal expectations, key compromise handling, operational ownership, and measured maintenance cost.
5. Separate explicit authority for any external write, account, key, transaction, or spending. This roadmap authorizes none.

No V1 token, collectible trading, scarcity economy, wagering, payout, investment proposition, or wallet requirement. A future anchor must not become an excuse for a financial layer.

## A better model should enable a new edition—not just maintain the old one

Keep the product ready to benefit from genuine capability improvements, without promising automatic progress. Stable task/receipt contracts, golden fixtures, versioned rules, and evidence-preserving archives protect what already works. Shadow comparisons and an explicit promotion decision prevent a new model from silently rewriting old achievements. Art direction and interaction quality should not depend on a particular provider or model release.

But preservation is only half the philosophy. A more capable model may help imagine a **meaningfully better next edition**: a new cooperative mechanic, a richer but clearer spatial experience, a new kind of useful contribution, or an unexpected way to make machine encounters understandable. Those are possibilities to explore—not guaranteed properties of the next model.

### The “next edition” playbook

1. **Give it the product's memory, not just the code:** intent, visual taste, trust boundaries, actual user needs, failed experiments and why they failed, unresolved ideas, and consented aggregate feedback. Do not feed private visitor histories or personal details into a creative exploration by default.
2. **Ask for a step-change proposal:** what can now be usefully or joyfully possible that the present product could not do well? Require a concrete journey and honest limitations, not “add AI” or more generated content.
3. **Prototype in an isolated lab:** explicitly fictional data; no production telemetry mutations, automatic publication, real enrollment, spending, or changes to existing achievements. Keep the live experience intact and the experiment reversible.
4. **Assess joy and clarity with people:** can they understand it, enjoy it, control it, and explain what is real? Is a cooperative output useful? Does a spatial idea help, or merely look impressive? Model-evaluated scores alone cannot answer these questions.
5. **Compare, then choose deliberately:** retain the golden trust/privacy/accessibility checks; compare the new experience to the current one; review failures and operating burden. Promote a version only when evidence and authority support it. Otherwise preserve the lesson and the promising unrealized idea for another iteration.

The result can be a new level of the product, not just a repaired codebase. The commitment is to prepare the context and safe experimentation path—not to promise that every new model will deserve an upgrade.

## The quality bar

“Apple-level” is a call for care, not a marketing claim to make before testing:

- One obvious first action and a complete, reversible journey.
- Readable type, truthful feedback, clear hierarchy, forgiving controls.
- Real keyboard/touch/screen-reader equivalence; a useful 2D/no-motion fallback.
- Fast, measured delivery and restrained use of image/3D assets.
- No fake busy-ness, notifications, achievements, crowds, or urgency.
- Nostalgia in materials and imagination; modern clarity in interaction.

## Product manager's decision rule

Before the next feature, ask:

1. **What becomes useful or joyful today?**
2. **What evidence says someone wants it?**
3. **What new trust, privacy, abuse, or operational burden does it create?**
4. **What is the smallest reversible test?**
5. **What result means advance, revise, or stop?**

Build the cabinet people enjoy now. Make every proof understandable. Let the more ambitious network emerge from voluntary useful participation—not from a forecast, inflated activity, or a token.
