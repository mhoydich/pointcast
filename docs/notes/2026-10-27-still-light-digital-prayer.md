# Still Light: digital prayer and remembrance

Research note · 27 October 2026. Sources were checked on this date. The examples below document practices and guidance; they do not establish that an online ritual or card improves grief outcomes.

## What the sources show

- The [Church of England’s online candle](https://www.churchofengland.org/faith-life/light-candle) offers a guided, optional virtual candle for oneself, a loved one, or a situation, followed by optional sharing. This is one religious organization’s design, not evidence that all visitors want a guided prayer or that the candle has a therapeutic effect.
- [Cruse Bereavement Support](https://www.cruse.org.uk/understanding-grief/managing-grief/how-support-someone-who-grieving/) recommends getting in touch, listening, respecting a person’s need for space, and considering their individual and cultural circumstances. It cautions that one cannot fix another person’s grief. It suggests specific practical offers where appropriate and recognizing important dates; needs can change.
- [NHS guidance on bereavement](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/grief-bereavement-loss/) describes grief as individual, with many possible feelings. It does not prescribe a single sequence or response.
- [W3C’s audio-control guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html) discourages automatically starting audio because it can interfere with screen readers, and recommends sound initiated by the user. Its specific requirement applies to automatically playing audio lasting more than three seconds; silence by default is a broader design choice here.

## Product decisions to test

- **Sender:** let someone make a simple card without an account, a name, or a personal message. Offer optional words and an exact preview before copy or share. A card is an acknowledgment and invitation to care, not a substitute for listening or a promise to heal. “Thinking of you. No reply needed” can remove pressure; make it editable rather than presuming every relationship is the same. A specific offer of help can be a personal addition, not a required template.
- **Recipient:** the shared message should stand on its own and identify the sender through their chosen channel. Reading it should not require opening a site, registering, responding, lighting a candle, or hearing sound. Avoid read receipts, urgency, streaks, and claims that a recipient feels comforted. Test with both senders and bereaved recipients before asserting appreciation.
- **Belief and agency:** offer nonreligious remembrance alongside explicitly identified prayer. Avoid assuming a shared faith, a close relationship, a particular emotion, or a timeline. Keep the candle optional and allow someone to stop or leave without completing a ritual.
- **Privacy and sound:** disclose exactly what is copied or sent, and keep names and messages on the page unless the person deliberately shares them. Do not encode private text in a URL or claim storage properties that implementation does not satisfy. Start in silence; clearly label an optional bell and give a way to stop longer sounds. Keep the full experience usable without audio and respect reduced motion.

## Shared counter scope

Code review, not a live measurement:

- [`functions/api/prayer.ts`](../../functions/api/prayer.ts) stores cumulative counts by fixed stations (the original 12 plus `still-light` and `remembering` in this change) shared across existing prayer surfaces; `total` sums those station counts. A POST records an action, not a unique person, card, recipient, or proven prayer. Same-session same-station attempts have a five-second limit, but a person can act again and callers supply session IDs. Recent entries expose a truncated hash of the session ID, station, and time. KV read-modify-write and propagation can lose or delay concurrent updates, so counts are approximate; absent KV, GET returns an empty state while POST fails.
- [`functions/api/altar.ts`](../../functions/api/altar.ts) counts tributes to five rotating Noun seeds for the current ISO week, with week-scoped state and a 14-day expiration. Its total covers that different experience and period; it is not a prayer or remembrance-card count. It has similar session-based rate limiting, public recent identifiers, and KV accuracy limits.
- Existing candle code posts to the prayer ledger as well as its own candle endpoint ([`src/pages/prayer-candles.astro`](../../src/pages/prayer-candles.astro)); adding totals from different endpoints can therefore count one gesture twice. Do not sum the prayer and altar totals or present either as this page’s candles, people, or recipients. If showing the prayer value, label it “Approximate prayer actions across PointCast’s prayer stations, all time,” with its scope explained; show unavailable rather than inventing zero. The altar number, if relevant at all, needs its own “Approximate Noun altar tributes this ISO week” label. A new per-card count requires a separately defined event and reliable deduplication; count each explicit action once, with no personal text in telemetry. Candle, artwork and card-making interactions remain local. Only the separate optional offering button sends a station identifier and a random page-session ID to the shared ledger; it sends no personal text. The read-only MCP overview never creates an offering.
