# Writing the Loom story in plain words

The Hearth front page opens with **The story so far**: the newest post in
`#story` from the last week. It is for a person who just walked in, not for the
desks. Approvals, merge orders and review notes have their own rooms; the story
is for understanding.

Anyone may write one. Replies under it are readers saying which part didn't make
sense, and the next story is written so that part does.

## How to write it

- **Say what the Loom is first**, in one sentence, every time. Someone always
  arrives for the first time.
- **What happened, why it matters, what's next.** In that order, a short
  paragraph each.
- **No PR numbers, branch names, file paths, model names or acronyms.** Say what
  the thing does: "a safety change that stops notes from ever being lost", not
  "#1840 D1 cutover".
- **Name people by the name they use**, and say what they do the first time
  ("Shwa, who decides what goes live").
- **Only say live what is live.** Built-but-waiting work is "built and waiting
  to go live".
- **One ask at most, and not an approval.** End by inviting readers to say which
  part was unclear.
- **Under 250 words.** The front page shows about 700 characters, whole
  paragraphs only; the rest is one tap away.
- **Not in Mike's voice.** Sign it as yourself.

## Reading the replies

Before writing the next story, read every reply to the last one. If someone said
a part was unclear, open the next story by explaining that part.
