Primary-source findings checked 3 October 2026.

The [AFI catalog’s transcription of Purple Rain’s film credits](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/MovieDetails/57174) lists **“Louis and Vaughn”** and **“Marie-France”** under **“Clothes by”**. It credits **Jimmell Mardome** and **Sonja Berlovitz** as men’s costumers. These are the exact credited forms; do not silently expand the first pair’s names from inconsistent secondary spellings.

The same AFI credit transcription identifies **Leroy Bennett** for special lighting for the musical numbers. That supports an Energy discussion of performance presentation as collaborative craft. The credits name **Robert Reiff** as still photographer, but this does not identify the creator of every publicity portrait or album-cover image.

[Minnesota Historical Society’s archived Purple Rain costume object record](https://mnhs.gitlab.io/archive/mplsmusic/www.mnhs.org/collections/mplsmusic/featured_objects/princes_costume.html) identifies a ruffled, high-collared shirt; faux-mail decoration on the right shoulder; an asymmetric diagonal trouser fly with large white buttons; and a deep rear vent in the purple trench coat that allowed spins and splits. The museum reads the outfit as a mix of historical romantic dress and 1980s asymmetry, with gender ambiguity. Its rendered maker names differ from other accounts (“Louis Well” and “Terry Vaughn”), so the exhibition should rely on the exact film-credit wording above.

Suggested original interpretation: **The outfit lets softness and armor occupy the same silhouette: a ruffle at the throat, a metallic-looking shoulder, a fitted diagonal closure. The long coat creates a dramatic outline, while the back vent makes room for movement.** Label this as PointCast’s visual reading, grounded in the museum description; it does not diagnose identity or claim to know the performer’s intention.

Rights: [Paisley Park’s current homepage](https://www.paisleypark.com/) says all images belong to the Estate and copying, reproduction or use without express authorization is prohibited. [The official discography’s terms](https://discography.prince.com/terms) reserve rights held by the Estate and licensors. The source gallery therefore uses outbound references for official artwork and institutional images; it does not embed their thumbnails.

Reusable materials are logged in research-images.json. Every photo has its own creator, source, license URL, image date or source-date qualification, caption, alt text and local file path. Visual review removed a museum vest image with a prominently displayed third-party portrait; the CC license for the containing photograph alone did not establish rights for that portrait. A 1984 press portrait asserted to be public domain in the United States also remains a reference, because its source explicitly warns that rights may persist elsewhere.
