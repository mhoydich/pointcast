## California sagebrush

*Artemisia californica* · Asteraceae · Southern California native

**Field ID.** An aromatic shrub with slender branches and finely divided gray-green leaves. Look for it in dry coastal sage scrub. Its habitat supports birds and small mammals, including communities used by the California gnatcatcher. [USDA NRCS plant guide](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/plantmaterials/capmcpg9970.pdf)

**Documented traditional use.** The Muwekma Ohlone Native Plant Garden entry at Santa Clara University records historical use for wounds and sore teeth. [Ohlone Heritage Hub](https://www.scu.edu/community-heritage-lab/ohlone-heritage-hub/ohlone-native-plant-garden/california-sagebrush/)

**Medicinal evidence · Preliminary.** A small community study tested a topical liniment. Its fixed treatment order and multi-ingredient formula make it hard to tell how much of the reported pain response came from sagebrush itself. [Community study, 2020](https://npaa.in/journal-ijta/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/1209IJTA_20_47-50.pdf)

**Metaphysical reading · Endurance.** Look at how much the plant does with such fine leaves. What could you make lighter during a dry season?

**Name check.** The scientific name matters here. Kitchen sage belongs to a different genus, and California mugwort has its own field card below. [NCCIH on common sage](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/sage)

## California buckwheat

*Eriogonum fasciculatum* · Polygonaceae · Southern California native

**Field ID.** Many thin branches carry clusters of narrow leaves. Its white to pink flowers form rounded or flat-topped clusters and supply nectar through a long bloom season. Watch one flower head for two minutes and count the different visitors. [USDA NRCS plant guide](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/pg_erfa2.pdf)

**Documented traditional use.** The USDA guide records Cahuilla historical uses for headache and stomach pain. [USDA NRCS ethnobotany section](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/pg_erfa2.pdf)

**Medicinal evidence · Historical record.** This starter review found no direct human clinical evidence for those uses of this species. Common food buckwheat belongs to the genus *Fagopyrum*. [USDA NRCS plant guide](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/pg_erfa2.pdf)

**Metaphysical reading · Reciprocity.** One flower is small. The whole cluster can keep a lot of visitors fed. What small contribution could you repeat long enough for someone else to rely on it?

## California mugwort

*Artemisia douglasiana* · Asteraceae · Western North American native

**Field ID.** An upright aromatic perennial, often along drainages or riparian corridors. Compare the green upper surface of a leaf and its pale, hairy underside. Include both in a photo record. [USDA NRCS plant guide](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/cs_ardo3.pdf)

**Cultural record.** The Ohlone-led mak-’amham project publishes a first-person plant story connecting *hiišen* with sleep and dreams. That account is specific to its authors and community. [Mak-’amham / Cafe Ohlone](https://www.makamham.com/hiisen)

**Medicinal evidence · Historical record.** The sources reviewed do not establish a human sleep benefit for this species. The USDA guide flags thujone toxicity and advises against ingesting mugwort during pregnancy. [USDA NRCS safety and ethnobotany](https://plants.sc.egov.usda.gov/DocumentLibrary/plantguide/pdf/cs_ardo3.pdf)

**Metaphysical reading · Thresholds.** The two sides of the leaf suggest two ways to see the same thing. Keep a notebook by the bed and write down one detail you remember on waking.

## Peppermint

*Mentha × piperita* · Lamiaceae · Cultivated hybrid

**Field ID.** Look for opposite toothed leaves and the familiar cooling scent in a labeled garden plant. Use the source label or a documented identification to distinguish it from other mints. [NC State Extension plant profile](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/mentha-x-piperita/)

**Documented use.** Mint plants have a long history of use for digestive symptoms. [NCCIH peppermint oil fact sheet](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/peppermint-oil)

**Medicinal evidence · Limited clinical support.** Research suggests short-term symptom benefit in some adults with irritable bowel syndrome from enteric-coated peppermint **oil capsules**. Evidence for peppermint **leaf** is insufficient. The oil may cause heartburn or indigestion. [NCCIH evidence and safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/peppermint-oil)

**Metaphysical reading · Clarity.** Use the familiar association with freshness as a writing prompt. What is one thing you could clear off your desk before you go home?

## Add a field note

Choose a labeled garden specimen or an observation from a public path. Photograph the full plant, a close-up of both leaf surfaces, and a flower or seed head when present. Record the observation date, broad location, habitat, and photographer. Keep sensitive locations broad.

```text
Working name / scientific name:
Date / broad location:
ID confidence: tentative | reviewed | confirmed by named source
What I saw:
Photo and credit:
What helped identify it:
Cultural source or study, if any:
My own association:
Open question for the next observer:
```

When adding a medicinal claim, record the plant part, preparation, whether it was tested in people, the specific outcome, and a source. Keep uncertainty visible. For a cultural account, link to the public source and name the community that shared it. 

**First field question.** Where does the walk transition from dry scrub to damp ground? Photograph one leaf on either side before giving either plant a name.
