## 2. The story people are being invited into

The public promise is small and specific: **a little printshop where unfinished things and unexpected guests are welcome.**

The central characters carry different kinds of affection:

- **Barley:** a horse whose patience is observable in action. His presence gives scenes a quiet center.
- **Bridle:** the attentive keeper who notices what the horse needs. Horse behavior should stay grounded in observation and care.
- **Hem:** the established poet in the black beret. He brings wit and a slightly crooked eye.
- **Crumb:** the bread maker who turns an arithmetic problem into hospitality.
- **The apprentice:** a maker learning that imperfect work can still matter to someone. His unchosen name gives readers a specific way to participate.

These are creative hypotheses to test with readers, not claims about which horse stories are statistically most popular. The first two stories test patience, companionship, shared craft, and gentle comic problem-solving. Later stories can test an orchard journey or Barley's encounter with a paper moon without abandoning that recognizable emotional world.

Keep the garden's visual language consistent: warm windows, violet evenings, paper and ink, California palms, jacarandas, bread, and a chestnut horse at the gate. Preserve each character's proportions and identifying features. The cozy village feeling can carry the earlier “hobbit” brief through food, gardens, and hospitality while the setting and characters remain our own.

**The first shared reader invitation:** “What would you name the apprentice?” Ask for a reason as well as a name when there is room. A real answer may inform a later story; credit the contributor and obtain permission before incorporating distinctive submitted text or artwork.

Character animation is a separate later experiment.

**Next: two authored paths in the reading room.** “Stay at the gate” gives Barley's view; “Follow the warm bread” gives Crumb's. Both return to the garden. Music begins on a tap, all text works silently, and motion has a still option. This is proposed development, not an existing feature.

**Then: a letter from the garden.** A complete chapter, an image, one short musical passage, and one question become the repeatable publication unit. A narrated edition is an optional later step once an original narrator and production method are chosen.

The production test is whether image, sound, and action deepen the same feeling. More effects alone do not establish a recognizable world. Reuse the finished assets for the first test. | Reading/listening completion | An explicit reader report or verified instrumentation; current reading-room analytics are not implemented |
