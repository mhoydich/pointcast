The first issue

At a folding table near Dockweiler, a producer records ten seconds of shoreline sound, then asks one neighbor what they hear here that they couldn’t hear a block inland. A host introduces the location, plays the cleared answer, and closes the five-minute piece. The proposed edition pairs a playable PointCast page with a transcript and one print-ready bulletin, subject to the participant’s choices.

There’s already a public workroom, a rehearsal script by Fax from the Office Noun Temp Pool, a field sheet with four separate permission choices, and an editable 3D cabin. The producer seat and first recording window are open. A working vehicle is a later project.

Who might pay

Start with a neighborhood retailer that already buys local promotion and an independent event organizer with an audience arriving in person. A shop could underwrite an edition about its block and carry the bulletin by the register. An organizer could commission the sound of one afternoon and give attendees the finished page to share.

Test one sponsor per edition at $750. The proposed package includes a named acknowledgment, a shareable episode page, a print-ready bulletin, and a delivery receipt. That receipt would link the published assets and record the actual print quantity and pickup locations. Ask the buyer which of these they value and who controls the budget. Reach and an audience size still need measurement.

What backing would buy

  1. Produce three editions.

    Support producer time, basic recording gear and a small print run. Publish three consent-cleared pilots, each with a public issue page and a delivery receipt. Record the cost and time for each.

  2. Test buyer demand.

    Fund interviews and sales follow-up. Interview ten prospective buyers, secure two paid $750 pilots from unrelated buyers, then obtain one repeat order. Record the price actually paid and the reason each buyer bought or passed.

  3. Price the vehicle separately.

    Commission a professional feasibility review and costed quotes before any fabrication. Compare access, transport, staffing, storage and insurance against the cost of taking the table and microphones to each recording.

These are proposed milestones. Paid sponsorship, repeat business, audience reach and production cost are still to be demonstrated. A funding amount and investment terms have yet to be announced.

Open questions

  • Which buyer can sign for $750, and what would make them order a second issue?
  • How many producer hours and printed copies does an issue actually use? How many people listen or take a copy?
  • Can a local producer consistently secure a place to record and permission for each use of a neighbor’s words?
  • Can a second producer repeat the format at a second venue without founder labor? What extra work would a tracked cabin win over a borrowed room, and could it earn back its added cost?
