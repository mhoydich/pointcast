What is in a ping

Four parts. The first is numbers. The other three are what make it worth answering.

PartWhat it holds
SignalsA few graded figures: residents, jobs posted, postings per 100 households, the largest job family.
NeedsWhat the town is short of, a next step, and how sure the reading is.
AsksWhat the town wants to know and does not. Someone else may already have it.
NeighborsThe towns next door, so a reader can walk from one ping to the next.

Build one for your town

The work is written up as a brief an AI agent can follow start to finish. You give it the brief and the name of a town.

  1. Copy the brief. It covers where to find the figures, how to count jobs, how to grade every number and what the ping looks like.
  2. Hand it to an agent with a town name. Claude, ChatGPT or any agent that can search the web and write files.
  3. Have someone local read it. The agent writes two or three readings of what the town needs. A resident will know in a minute if one is wrong.
  4. Publish and send the address. Put the files on any host and send the address of your ping to mh@pointcast.xyz to be listed.

Where this could go

None of these exist yet. They are what becomes possible once many towns publish the same small file.

IdeaTowns answering each other

El Segundo's ping says engineers crowd out every other kind of opening. Torrance's shows three times the technician work. A reader that holds both can point one at the other without anyone running a meeting.

IdeaA weather map for work

Ping every town in a county once a month and watch where hiring thickens and thins, the way you would watch a storm front.

IdeaAsks that find their answer

Every ping lists what its town does not know. A school district, a transit agency or a neighbor with the right spreadsheet can answer one ask without joining anything.

IdeaResidents pinging back

A check-in at a brewery, a weather reading from a porch, a pothole. Small signals from people, added to the town's file with the same grades.

IdeaAgents that subscribe

An assistant helping someone choose where to live, or a business choosing where to open, reads the pings instead of scraping ten city websites.

IdeaPooling by town

Each need comes with a next step. Residents and employers could pledge toward a step and see the pledge count on the ping.

IdeaSister towns far away

Medway, Massachusetts and a Queens neighborhood on the same network as Hermosa Beach. Towns that look alike in their pings can compare notes across the country.
