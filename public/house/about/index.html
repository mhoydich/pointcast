HOUSE CHANNEL · V0pointcast

The TV as the hearth

A TV is dark most of the day. House Channel is one web page that gives it something quiet to do when nobody is watching anything, and a second page that turns a phone into its remote.

There is no account and no app. The TV shows five letters. You type them on a phone. That is the whole setup.

How to put it on a TV

  • Open pointcast.xyz/house in the TV's own browser, if it has one.
  • Or open it on a laptop and send that tab or screen to the TV, or plug the laptop in with a cable.
  • Press OK or click once for full screen. The page asks the screen to stay awake where the browser allows it.

Four views

  • Hearth. A drawn fire. Stack the wood on the phone, press and hold to light it, press and hold again to breathe on it. Drag the lid down to snuff it. It burns down on its own in 15, 45 or 90 minutes, so it also keeps time for a dinner or a game.
  • Window. The sky, cloud, wind and tide for a place on the South Bay coast, read from the same weather and tide sources the rest of PointCast uses. When those cannot be read, the TV shows a simulated sky you set by hand, and labels it as simulated.
  • Lights. A hillside of small windows, one for each person in a Light Companion circle. Paste a circle link on the phone and the windows catch in turn. If the circle service can't be reached, the TV shows a demo circle and says it is a demo.
  • Drum. Every paired phone becomes a set of pads in a PointCast drum room. The TV shows the room and makes the sound.

Manners

  • The TV starts silent. Sound comes only after a phone asks for it, and only from the TV. If the TV's browser wants a press on the TV first, both screens say so.
  • Off is a black screen. Nothing is drawn.
  • An idle TV dims after twenty minutes. A burning fire is not idle.
  • End, on the phone, blanks the TV and forgets the pairing on both screens.
  • Everything on screen drifts slowly so nothing sits on the same spot for hours.

What is kept

For pairing, the site keeps the five letters and the name of a room for ten minutes, and a record that the room exists for six hours at most. It keeps no name, no address and nothing about what is on the TV. The fire, the sky and the settings live in the TV's browser tab and go away with it. A circle link you paste goes to the TV for that session and is not written down.

What it is not

It does not control the TV itself: not its power, input or volume buttons. It does not talk to lights or other devices in the house. This is version zero and it has been checked in a desktop browser, not yet on real TVs.

For agents and builders: /house.json describes the pages, the pairing endpoint and the frames. Research on what a plain web page can do for the home is in the repository at docs/research/2026-11-11-web-home-capabilities.md.
