Three numbers.

The buoy measures a sea state. Our drawing makes a few relationships visible.

Go deeper in the Pacific field guide ↗
01 / HEIGHT

A sea state, not a single wave.

Significant wave height is a statistical measure, roughly the average height of the highest one-third of waves. Individual waves vary; some are larger.

NDBC measurement method ↗

02 / PERIOD

Give the ocean its own clock.

Dominant period is the period with the most wave energy. The animation uses that many seconds between crests at a fixed point. Longer periods imply wider spacing in the approximate deep-water model.

Model wavelength and speed will appear when period is available.

03 / DIRECTION

From there. Moving this way.

The buoy reports where waves come from, in degrees true. Their travel bearing is 180° opposite. The north-up compass and surface show that direction when it is available.

A study of offshore waves. The drawn surface is original, approximate geometry, not a camera view or a reconstruction of the waves’ actual phase. The deep-water relationship uses wavelength ≈ gT² / 2π and phase speed ≈ gT / 2π. Shore depth, refraction, tide and breaking can change the picture at the beach. This is not a beach surf forecast or a prediction of swimming safety.

PointCast age policy: fresh ≤ 90 minutes · delayed ≤ 3 hours · stale ≤ 6 hours · expired after 6 hours. Age follows the observation time. Expired observations remain dated and motion stops. Refreshing does not make an old observation new.
