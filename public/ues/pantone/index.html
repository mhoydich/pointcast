The question

How do you send
a color, reliably?

Pantone is a commercial system for naming, communicating and reproducing color. The useful lesson extends beyond a swatch: a shared name works best with a reference, a material, a production method and a decision about what counts as acceptable.

By the end, you should be able to explain why identical screen values can look different, distinguish display color from spot and process printing, and specify a proof before production.

Start with the PointCast history and color labs. For the production distinction, see Adobe’s spot and process documentation.

Your route through the studio

Observe. Translate. Hand off.

Use any paper you already have, or work entirely on screen. Buying a guide, printing a sample or contacting a supplier is not required for this exercise.

  1. 01

    10 minutes / Observe

    Question your eyes.

    Open the context lab. Keep the center color fixed and change its surroundings. Record what appears to change, then separate that observation from the underlying value.

  2. 02

    10 minutes / Translate

    Change the medium.

    Compare spot ink, process printing and a display. Imagine your design moving from a phone to paper, then fabric. Write down which assumptions need to be checked again.

  3. 03

    25 minutes / Make + reflect

    Build the agreement.

    Use an original palette, answer the three questions below, then make a brief. End with a small proof request and one uncertainty you would resolve before production.

A next experiment

Make an agreement
someone can repeat.

Try a local materials library: give three found, permitted materials their own sample IDs, record where you observed them and under what light, then ask another person to interpret your notes. This is a proposed exercise, not an existing collection or a manufacturing standard.

For an optional partner critique, compare the two interpretations. Revise the brief where words or values left room for an expensive guess. Sharing is your choice; this page has no submission channel.
