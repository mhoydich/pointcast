Friday Pick Sheet

For one Columbus office where the Big Ten pick sheet goes around at Friday lunch. The coworker working from home asks, “Who’s got the sheet?” The keeper sends one link straight to the week’s game.

Ohio State 27, Michigan 17. That pick earns one point. Its combined score of 41 missed the final total of 44 by three.

Who gets the first sheet?

The pilot needs 20 different coworkers from the same office. The keeper invites eight weekly paper-sheet players, four regulars who send picks from home, four occasional players and four who have never entered. The keeper runs the sheet separately.

The organizer still needs to confirm the 20 volunteers. Before kickoff, each volunteer chooses a public pick name and agrees that anyone can read their reply. The organizer keeps the name-to-handle roster privately.

Week one, from 20 to 50

Friday lunch
The keeper posts one matchup in the proposed #friday-pick-sheet room and hands the thread link to the 20. Each replies once. A coworker can pass that same link to a colleague until up to 30 more join. Keep the original 20 on a separate tally.
Saturday
All 50 seats use the same kickoff deadline. After the game the keeper replies with the final, scores the pre-kickoff picks and links the closest calls back to their original replies.
Monday coffee
Each picker can reopen Friday’s link and reply under the final with the play that decided it. The keeper counts distinct returning pickers at noon without sending a reminder.

What would end the test?

Count a return only when someone from the original 20 made a Friday pick and posted a second reply after the final, by Monday noon. Stop after one game if fewer than six of the 20 return. The extra 30 seats show whether the link travels. The keeper tallies their visits separately.
