SB Surf House a study

What it would take for a community to research, buy, design and run a shared surf house on the Santa Barbara coast, with open books and a public-good share. The research and design were done by Claude, an AI model, at Mike Hoydich's request. Nothing was bought and no house has been chosen.

Plainly

  • This is a study and a public resource. It is not an offer.
  • Nothing is being bought or sold. No money is being raised. No entity has been formed, and no house, land or animal has been chosen.
  • It is not legal, tax, health or investment advice.
  • Tradable shares in property are likely securities. Anyone going near that needs a securities lawyer first. Why.

The place

A coast that faces south and wakes up in the fall

As of Nov 11, 2026

This coast runs east to west, and the Channel Islands sit in front of it. They block the summer south swells that reach Malibu, so the breaks here are close to flat from June to August and come alive with the first west swells in September and October. The Surf Atlas guide A surf house here has a season. Winter is for surfing. Summer is for repairs, the garden and teaching kids.

Wave notes are from one surf guide. The Surf Atlas guide The house suggestions are this study's design judgement.

Three kinds of neighbourhood, three kinds of house

Walk-to-break streets

The Mesa above Leadbetter and Mesa Lane, and the beach blocks of Carpinteria near Tar Pits. Small lots, close neighbours. A bungalow for six, bikes instead of cars, quiet by ten.

The student area

Isla Vista, beside Campus Point, Devereux and Sands. It is unincorporated County land with its own pressures: a County inspection pilot found 23 of the first 60 properties needed corrective work, and a new County standard counts one person per 70 sq ft bedroom, with 50 sq ft more for a second. Independent, Dec 15 2025 A larger shared house fits here, on an inland street.

The family beach town

Carpinteria, with a gentle beach break and Rincon at the county line. A house with a small second unit for a resident host suits a place where visitors are counted and capped. Carpinteria code 14.47, third-party copy Read on a third-party copy of the city code, not the City's own page.

What the coast itself rules out

  • Bluff tops. About 80 buildings sit on the Isla Vista bluff along Del Playa. County policy calls for a geotechnical study at 15 feet from the edge and vacating the affected part of a building at 10 feet; 30 of those buildings have already been cut back, and a 2024 balcony collapse displaced 45 tenants. Independent, Sep 22 2026 An earlier County presentation put retreat at up to 20 inches a year in places. Independent, Jul 19 2019 A community house does not belong on the edge.
  • Low waterfront and the Mesa bluffs. The City plans around 0.8 ft of sea-level rise by 2030, 2.5 ft by 2060 and 6.6 ft by 2100. At 2.5 ft, bluff erosion reaches bluff-top neighbourhoods and most sandy beaches below the westerly bluffs are likely lost; at 6.6 ft, roughly 1,250 parcels are affected. City SLR plan summary, 2021 City sea-level page
  • Fire zones. The 2025 state maps put parts of the Mesa near Cliff Drive, and northern San Roque and La Cumbre, in high or very high hazard zones; Carpinteria has little. New building in those zones must meet fire-resistant standards and properties get defensible-space inspections. Independent, Apr 13 2025
  • Insurance. The state says the insurer of last resort, the FAIR Plan, grew 1.9% in the second quarter of 2026, its slowest since 2022, and that insurers are returning. CA Dept. of Insurance, Aug 3 2026 A quote for the actual address, before any offer, is the only number that counts.

The rules

Four governments and the Coastal Commission

As of Nov 11, 2026. Rules are changing this season; read the linked pages before acting.

The coastal zone

Each city and county writes a Local Coastal Program of policies and zoning, and the Coastal Commission certifies it. After that the local government issues most coastal development permits, with some decisions appealable to the Commission. Coastal Commission Changes to a house near the shore can need a coastal permit on top of a building permit.

City of Santa Barbara

The Council adopted two short-term rental ordinances in October 2026. Inland, whole-home rentals are limited to housing in commercial zones and barred from residential zones and high-fire areas, with rollout planned for January 2027. In the coastal zone the ordinance limits them in single-family and two-family zones (two reports describe the limit differently) and only about 12% of coastal units could apply; it waits on Coastal Commission approval. Until then coastal rentals remain allowed under a 2021 appeals ruling. Independent, Oct 7 2026 Avalara, Oct 2026 City notice, Sep 2026

Three details matter for a club: one licence per owner, no licences for trusts or corporate entities, and no licence for an ADU. Quiet hours run 10 pm to 7 am and a manager must be able to reach the house within 30 minutes. Independent, Oct 7 2026 Avalara, Oct 2026

Not verified against the ordinance text. This study read a news report, a tax-service summary and the City's September notice, not the adopted ordinances, and the two summaries differ on single-family zones.

Santa Barbara County, including Isla Vista

Inland, renting for 30 days or less needs a County permit and is confined to certain zones; a homestay, with the owner or long-term tenant on site, is allowed in residential zones. In the County's coastal zone, short-term rentals and homestays are still unregulated by zoning, because the Coastal Commission rejected the County's 2018 coastal rules on a 7 to 5 vote; those rules would have left Isla Vista's student zones out. Operators everywhere owe a 14% transient occupancy tax. County release, Apr 1 2025 Coastal Commission, 2018 County page (did not load, not read)

Not verified on the County's own ordinance page, which loaded only its navigation on Nov 11, 2026. This paragraph rests on the County's April 2025 press release and a 2018 Coastal Commission staff report. Whether anything changed in the County's coastal zone since April 2025 is unconfirmed.

Carpinteria

Vacation rentals are allowed only inside an overlay district of four areas with licence caps of 55, 115, 30 and 18, a waiting list beyond that, and at most two occupants per unit plus two per bedroom. A homestay is allowed citywide in the owner's primary residence with the owner present, up to four guests and one car per booking. Carpinteria code 14.47, third-party copy City of Carpinteria, Jul 2025

The four cap numbers are not verified against the City's own code. They come from a third-party copy of the municipal code; the City's 2025 policy document confirms the structure but not the numbers.

Second units

State law requires cities to approve qualifying ADUs without a hearing, within 60 days where a house already exists. A detached unit of up to 800 sq ft with 4-foot side and rear setbacks cannot be blocked, a junior unit inside the house can be up to 500 sq ft, and one lot can combine a converted unit, a junior unit and a detached unit. The same law applies in the coastal zone without weakening Coastal Act protections, and units built under the state minimums must be rented for more than 30 days at a time. HCD ADU Handbook, Mar 2026

What this means for a members' house

  • A house that members use themselves is a home with many keys. A house that sells nights to the public is a short-term rental and needs a licence that, in the City, an entity cannot hold. The design here is the first kind.
  • Rotating use by many owners has been treated as a timeshare elsewhere in California. One wine-country city fought a fractional-home company in federal court and settled by capping it at four houses. Press Democrat In Santa Barbara, Riviera neighbours organised against one such house in 2022. Independent, May 24 2022 Zoning counsel has to answer this before any purchase.
  • Bedroom counts set the real capacity. The County's new standard is one person per 70 sq ft bedroom and 50 sq ft more for a second. Independent, Dec 15 2025 The plans below show small bunk rooms for that reason.
  • This study found no local rule written specifically for surf clubs or membership houses. That absence is a question for a land-use attorney, and it is listed under what could not be verified.

The house

A place with a wet door and a long table

Design concept. Nothing here describes a real property.

The house is for people who already live here. Locals come first: the nurse who surfs before a shift, the shaper's apprentice, the retired teacher with a longboard. Students get bunks and a workshop. Visiting friends get a bed when a member vouches for them. Nobody books it as a stranger.

The whole building is organised around one rule: sand and salt water never cross the kitchen. You arrive by the side gate, rinse under the outdoor shower, hang the suit, rack the board, and only then walk in dry. A surf hostel on Vancouver Island runs the same way, with an outdoor rinse shower and a heated shack for drying gear. Tofino Hostel FAQ

A week in November

MonDawn patrol leaves on bikes at first light. The swell bell rang at 5:40. Coffee is on the long table by eight, and the house is empty by nine.
TueWorkshop evening. Two members teach ding repair at the bench; a student fixes the board library's oldest log.
WedFlat. Cleaning rota, laundry, the monthly books go up on the wall and online.
ThuHouse meeting over dinner, forty minutes, one proposal: a second board rack or a better shower drain.
FriVisiting friends arrive. Wax the boards on the deck. Fire in the hearth until ten, then inside.
SatYouth surf morning: twelve local kids, loaner boards and suits from the library, members in the water with them.
SunBeach cleanup at low tide, then the shared table. Whoever cooked does not wash.

Staying a good neighbour

  • Quiet from 10 pm to 7 am, the same hours the City now writes into rental licences. Avalara, Oct 2026 Dawn patrol leaves without car doors.
  • Bikes and feet before cars. A set number of cars per night, written down.
  • One named member is on call each week and can be at the house within half an hour. Neighbours have the number.
  • The fire lives in a contained hearth with a lid, on gas or clean dry wood. The County requires a burn permit for outdoor burning other than campfires and cooking, and burning trash is illegal. County APCD / Fire No fire on red-flag days, and none at all if the address is in a fire hazard zone without the fire department's blessing.
  • No events, no amplified sound outside, shoes off inside. A coliving operator's standard rules read much the same: quiet after ten, register guests, nothing burning indoors, tidy for the next person. Outsite house rules
  • The first act in any neighbourhood is knocking on the six nearest doors with the house rules in hand.

Places

Presence without cameras

Design concept. Tap each signal.

A house shared by thirty people needs to say a few things about itself, and it should say them the way a porch does. Three signals, each one bit, each visible to members and to nobody else. No cameras, no location tracking, no names.

The light is a switch by the wet door. The rack has a simple sensor per slot, so a member across town can see that the nine-foot loaner is free. The bell rings once on mornings when the forecast crosses a threshold the members set. Everything else stays unrecorded on purpose.

Structures

Eight ways to own a house together

As of Nov 11, 2026. General descriptions, not advice on any of them.

Plain LLC with members

A company holds title; an operating agreement sets rights.

ForLenders, title companies and insurers understand it. Flexible rules. Limited liability.
AgainstInterests sold to passive members can be securities. Default rules favour capital over use unless rewritten. A City rental licence cannot go to an entity. Independent, Oct 7 2026

Housing cooperative

A cooperative corporation owns the building; members hold a share and a right to occupy. California defines a limited-equity form whose resale price is capped at the purchase price plus improvements and at most 10% a year. Cal. Civil Code 817

ForOne member, one vote. Built to stop speculation. Long local record: the student co-op in Isla Vista has owned houses since the 1970s. SBSHC
AgainstDesigned for residents, so part-time use needs careful drafting. Share loans are harder to find. Stock co-ops count as subdivisions under state law. TIC primer

Community land trust

A nonprofit owns the land for good and leases it, typically for 99 years, to whoever owns the building; a resale formula keeps it affordable; the board is split between leaseholders, neighbours and public representatives. Lincoln Institute

ForThe strongest public-good lock. Can accept gifts and grants. One long-running Vermont trust holds 679 homes after 40 years. Champlain Housing Trust
AgainstSlow to set up. Members never capture land appreciation, by design. Needs a charitable purpose a surf house may not meet alone.

Nonprofit club

A membership club, tax-exempt under section 501(c)(7) if it is funded mainly by dues, keeps membership limited and personal, and lets no earnings benefit an individual. Income from non-members is limited to 35% of receipts, 15% from use of the facilities. IRS: social clubs

ForMatches the use-only idea exactly: members pay dues and get the clubhouse. No shares to trade.
AgainstDues are not deductible gifts. Nobody gets equity back. A separate charity would be needed for donations to youth programmes.

Wyoming DAO LLC

An ordinary Wyoming LLC that declares itself a DAO, names its smart contract in its articles (within 30 days or it is dissolved), states how far it is run by code, and keeps a Wyoming registered agent. Filing costs $100. Wyoming Secretary of State

ForVotes on-chain have legal standing inside the company. Limited liability.
AgainstIt is still an LLC, so the securities question is unchanged. California lenders, escrow and insurers may balk. The one DAO that bought Wyoming land stalled. Cowboy State Daily, May 14 2023

Wyoming DUNA

A decentralized unincorporated nonprofit association, available since July 2024: at least 100 members, a nonprofit purpose, can hold real property in its own name, shields members from its debts, and cannot pay out profits. Law-firm note, Apr 2024

ForFits a large, open membership with no profit motive.
AgainstNew and lightly tested. One hundred members is a lot for one house. Unknown reception from California banks and title companies.

Tenancy in common

Each person holds a percentage of the deed itself; a private agreement, not recorded with the county, says who uses what. TIC primer

ForReal title for each owner. Individual loans exist in some markets.
AgainstWith a shared loan, one owner's default threatens all. Use rights rest on the agreement holding up. Offerings covering five or more residential units need a state public report. TIC primer Unworkable past a handful of owners.

Fractional vacation-home company

A company buys a house, puts it in an LLC, sells about eight shares and manages the calendar. Press Democrat

ForTurnkey. Financing and scheduling are solved.
AgainstBuilt for second homes of the well-off. Cities have treated it as a timeshare and neighbours have organised against it, here included. Press Democrat Independent, May 24 2022

And no wrapper at all? A DAO with no legal entity is the riskiest choice. A federal court treated one as an unincorporated association that can be sued and fined, and the regulator argued that anyone who voted its tokens could be personally liable. Proskauer, Jul 2023

Where this study lands. Start as a dues-paying members' club that rents. If the group lasts and wants to own, the cleanest shape is two layers: a nonprofit or land trust that holds the land and the public-good promise, and a members' club or cooperative that holds the use. One California cooperative already mixes resident, worker and community-investor members, with investors limited to a return of about 2%. Shareable, Aug 2018

The hard point

A tradable share in a house is probably a security

As of Nov 11, 2026. A plain-language summary, not legal advice.

US law asks one question of anything sold to raise money. Did people put in money, in a shared venture, expecting profit from someone else's work? If yes, it is an investment contract and the securities laws apply, whatever it is called and whatever chain it lives on. SEC staff framework (withdrawn)

What pushes a token toward being a security

Sold before the thing works. Marketed on price. A core team does the essential work. Sold in amounts no user would need. Freely transferable with a market to resell it. SEC staff framework (withdrawn)

What pushes it away

The thing already works and the token is used at once. Its value tracks the service, not a market. Transfers are limited to what use requires. The regulator's own example of a non-security was a non-transferable token redeemable only for a seller's goods at ordinary prices. SEC staff framework (withdrawn)

The page that set out those factors was a 2019 staff framework. It is now marked withdrawn, replaced on March 17, 2026 by a formal Commission interpretation that sorts crypto assets into five groups and presumes that tokens with a real function, called digital tools, are not securities. The underlying test did not change, the release creates no safe harbour, and courts are not bound by it. Law-firm summary, Mar 2026 SEC staff framework (withdrawn)

Not verified against the release itself. The March 2026 interpretation was read through a law-firm summary, not the Commission's own document.

The paths that exist

  • Use-only membership. Dues buy nights, storage and workshop time. The membership cannot be sold, pays nothing, and is refunded at most at cost. This is the path the whole design above assumes.
  • Accredited investors only. A private offering under Rule 506 has no dollar limit, but 506(c) requires verifying every buyer is accredited and the interests are restricted from resale. SEC exemptions chart
  • Regulated crowdfunding. Up to $5 million in twelve months, only through an SEC-registered portal or broker, with filed disclosures, limits on what ordinary investors can put in, and generally no resale for a year. SEC: Regulation Crowdfunding SEC exemptions chart
  • A registered or Regulation A offering. Possible on paper, up to $20 million or $75 million a year by tier, and far too heavy for one house. SEC exemptions chart

People often ask for tradable shares. A cautious reading is that members might swap nights with each other at no markup, and that ownership cannot be casually tradable at all. Whether even that much is lawful for a particular group is a lawyer's question. Any real version needs a securities lawyer and a California real-estate attorney before a single dollar or token changes hands.

What an AI can and cannot do in a purchase

Can

Research the market and the rules, read every disclosure and report, model the money, draft agendas, house rules, budgets and questions for counsel, compare structures, keep the books legible, schedule the calendar, and coordinate people.

Cannot

Sign, pay, borrow, hold title or be a member of record. A person or a legal entity does those. Acting as an agent in a sale or lease for pay requires a California broker licence. Cal. B&P Code 10131 Legal advice comes from lawyers, appraisals from appraisers, drawings for permit from licensed designers. Even a company run by code needs a filed charter and a registered agent. Wyoming Secretary of State

Precedents

What happened to the ones that tried

As of Nov 11, 2026. Each account is a paraphrase of the linked report. A group is named only where this page links its own site.

Santa Barbara Student Housing Co-op lasting

Founded by students in 1976, it owns five houses in Isla Vista with about 95 beds. An elected board sets the budget; residents run each house through meetings, chores and roles written into the lease. SBSHC Daily Nexus, May 22 2026

Champlain Housing Trust, Vermont lasting

Since 1984 the trust has kept homes affordable by sharing appreciation at resale: owners keep 25% of the gain. By early 2024 it held 679 homes that had served more than 1,320 owners. Champlain Housing Trust

A surfing club at San Onofre ended by public access

Formed in 1952 so surfers could keep access to a beach on a Marine base, it ran a locked gate, about 500 members and a five-year waiting list. Exclusive access ended in the early 1970s when the beach became a state park. LAist, Sep 7 2024

An online golf community and a Scottish links course bought through a company

An online golf community of about 5,400 voted 88% in favour of bidding and its club company bought a Scottish links course in 2023, guide price £750,000. Golf Business News, May 23 2023 The vote was collective; the buyer of record was a conventional company.

A land-buying DAO in Wyoming stalled

Raised about $8 million from more than 5,000 people and bought 40 acres near Yellowstone in 2021 for roughly $100,000. County zoning allows one house on the parcel, a fake land-drop scam took $95,000, votes needed 500 participants, and members never agreed on a use. Belfer Center, Jun 2023 Cowboy State Daily, May 14 2023 Cointelegraph, Sep 4 2025

Cabin DAO wound down

After about four years of residencies and coliving experiments, the group voted to stop funding governance, convert the treasury to dollars and return it to token holders. Its own conclusion: the community should be a loose network, and the DAO form had become a distraction. Cabin forum

A tokenised rental company in Detroit in court

Sold tokens in roughly 700 rental properties to mostly overseas buyers. The city sued over hundreds of code violations in 2025, a judge ordered rent into escrow for repairs, and in February 2026 the company told investors it could not carry on as before and suspended payouts. Michigan Public, Mar 20 2026

Fractional homes in California neighbours pushed back

See the rules section: a federal suit and a four-house cap in one city, and organised opposition on the Santa Barbara Riviera. Press Democrat Independent, May 24 2022

Five lessons

  • What lasts has a dull legal form, one vote per member, and work written into the deal.
  • Tokens raised money faster than the groups could agree what to do with it.
  • Zoning decides what a parcel can be. Read it before the white paper.
  • Distant holders plus thin local management ends with tenants and neighbours paying the price.
  • Exclusive access to a public coast does not hold. Build the public share in from the start.

What open means

The books, the rules, the plans, the minutes and this research are published where anyone can read them. Open does not mean anyone can book a bed.

What goes on a chain, and why so little

A public ledger is good at one thing a shared folder is not: proving later that a record was not changed. So the vote tallies and a monthly fingerprint of the books can be written to a chain, cheaply. Membership itself can be a non-transferable pass or a row in a spreadsheet; both work. Money, title and identity stay with banks, the county recorder and people. A group that cannot run on a spreadsheet will not be rescued by a contract.

Utility versus ownership

Utility is what a member gets: nights, a rack slot, workshop hours, a key to the wet door. Members can swap nights with each other at face value. Ownership is the deed and whatever equity sits under it. This design keeps the two apart, so that joining the house never means buying a piece of it.

The public-good share

A fixed slice of the house, a tenth in the calculator's default, belongs to people who are not members: free surf mornings for local youth, a board and wetsuit library, a monthly beach cleanup, the workshop open to anyone on Tuesdays. It is written into the founding rules so that a later vote cannot quietly remove it, and a land trust holding the ground would make it permanent.

Go to market, for a community

The first ten people and one rented weekend

Design concept

The first ten

People who already surf together at one break and live within a bike ride of it. Aim for a mix on purpose: two students, two tradespeople, a parent, a teacher, someone over sixty, someone who has run a co-op kitchen, someone who shapes or repairs, someone who does not surf but lives on the street.

The first meeting

One hour at a public picnic table after a morning session. Three questions only. What would you use? What would you give? What would make you leave? Notes go into a shared document that night.

The first weekend

Rent one lawfully licensed house, or book a group campsite, for two nights with the ten. Run it as the house would run: wet door, cleaning rota, quiet at ten, one shared dinner, one dawn patrol, and a youth morning if a parent can bring three kids. Split the bill ten ways and publish it. That is the whole test.

How to say what it is

A surf clubhouse that locals share. Members pay dues and get nights, a rack and a workbench. It is not an investment, there is nothing to buy, and the books are public. If someone asks about returns, the answer is none.

After the weekend, ask each person privately whether they would do it again next month. If fewer than seven say yes, the study has done its job cheaply.

What stays constant

  • No price is given for a specific house type. Small-house sale prices by neighbourhood, and Carpinteria figures newer than December 2025, were not found in an open source.

  • Wave descriptions come from a single surf guide.
  • The precedents are told from news reports, a case study and each group's own pages. None of the groups was contacted, and their position after the dates shown is not known.
