The rules, with the city's own pages

Short stays

Short-term rentals must be registered. A primary residence can host without limit when the registrant is on site, and for up to 90 days a registration period when not. Registrations for homes that are not a primary residence are capped at 800 citywide. ADUs cannot be short-term rentals. The page lists the fee as $400 in one place and $500 in another.

Source, read 2026-11-11: City of Long Beach: short-term rentals

Backyard units (ADUs)

Long Beach applies state ADU law directly while its own ordinance is rewritten. In the Coastal Zone an administrative local coastal development permit comes before the building permit.

Source, read 2026-11-11: City of Long Beach: ADUs

Coastal permits

The Local Coastal Program was certified in 1980. The City issues coastal permits in most of its coastal zone, some areas stay with the Coastal Commission, and City decisions in the appealable area can be appealed to the Commission.

Source, read 2026-11-11: City of Long Beach: coastal planning

Five questions for the planning counter

  1. Is this address inside the Coastal Zone, and who issues the coastal permit here?
  2. What is this parcel zoned, and is a club, meeting room or lodging use allowed in that zone?
  3. Is a stay under 30 days lawful at this address, and under which permit?
  4. What does the city count as an event or a gathering, and when does it need its own permit?
  5. Which business license and occupancy tax apply, and who has to hold them?
