The El Segundo house

A study of one small commons for a small town: a place ten neighbors keep together, with the books open and a share held back for everyone else. This page says what it could be, what the City of El Segundo allows, and how to begin without buying anything.

A study and a public resource, not an offer. There is no house, no listing and no fund. Nothing is for sale and no money is being raised. Not legal, tax or investment advice. The plain box.

What the house could be

A sketch by Claude, not a plan and not a listing.

Not a beach house, because El Segundo's sand is a short walk below a working shoreline and the town lives up on the bluff. Think of a town house for the water: an ordinary place a few blocks up from Grand Avenue with five jobs.

  • Board room. A rack of shared boards and wetsuits, a rinse hose, a ding-repair bench. Ride the bike path north to Dockweiler or south to El Porto.
  • Workshop. One good bench and the tools nobody needs to own alone.
  • Kitchen table. A long table and a big pot, for the supper after.
  • Music corner. Instruments on the wall, quiet hours on the door.
  • Garden strip. Herbs, tomatoes, a compost bin, a bench facing west.

It stays small on purpose: ten members to begin, each with a right to use the place and a duty to look after it, none with a stake to sell.

The same recipe, fitted to El Segundo

The first ten people
Ten who live or work in town. One seat, one voice. A waiting list rather than a bigger table.
A rented weekend first
In El Segundo the rented weekend has to change shape. The City's permit covers a resident owner renting bedrooms while living there, with at most six guests, so ten people cannot simply book a house for two nights. Step 2 on the ladder lists three lawful ways to get the same test.
House rules
One page. Quiet hours, guests, cleaning, who holds the key, how to leave.
Open books
One ledger that every member, and any neighbor who asks, can read. Every dollar in and out, with a name and a date.
Use-rights, not ownership
Members hold a turn at the place, not a piece of it. A turn cannot be sold or passed on for money. If someone leaves, the seat goes to the waiting list.
A public-good share
A fixed slice of the calendar and the budget belongs to the town: free youth surf mornings with loaner boards, an open repair bench, a supper anyone can join.

What El Segundo allows, from the City's own pages

Short stays

The City runs a home share permit. A homeowner may rent bedrooms for 30 days or less in the home they live in, and must be living there during the stay. The page caps guests at six on the parcel, bars parties and events, bars short stays in an ADU, and lists a permit registration fee of $396 and a renewal fee of $201. A whole house rented to a group for a weekend with no owner present is not what this permit covers.

Source, read 2026-11-11: City of El Segundo: home share rental permit

Backyard units (ADUs)

The City says ADUs are allowed by right in any zone that allows housing, with standards in Municipal Code sections 15-4E-3 (ADUs) and 15-4F-2 (JADUs). It offers an application handout, a fee calculator and pre-approved plans.

Source, read 2026-11-11: City of El Segundo: ADU and JADU resources

Coastal permits

The planning applications page lists a Coastal Development application guide. A third-party copy of Municipal Code Title 15, Chapter 26 says a coastal development permit is needed before development in the Coastal Zone and that the Planning Commission grants it. The adopted code itself was not opened.

What that means for a commons house

  • A whole house booked by a group for a weekend with no resident owner is not something the home share permit covers.
  • A backyard unit cannot be the short-stay part of the plan.
  • A stay of more than 30 days is a different thing from a short-term rental. Whether a month-long lease to a group, used on weekends, fits a given address is a question for the lease, the owner and the City. It was not verified here.
  • A clubhouse with no beds is a land-use question: what the parcel is zoned and whether a club or meeting use is allowed there. The zoning map was not opened, so that is not verified either.

The step ladder

Tick steps as a group works through them. The ticks stay in this browser and go nowhere.

  1. Ten people who live or work in town sit down once. No money. Each says what they would use a shared place for and what they would give to it.

  2. The city permits hosted rooms only, so the first weekend is not a rented house. Three ways to do it within the rules as read: a month-long lease used on weekends, campsites and a fire ring at Dockweiler next door, or a hosted stay in a member's permitted home for a few at a time. Costs are split in the open.

  3. Write one page of rules and keep one ledger that every member can read. Use the templates on the pattern page.

  4. Repeat monthly for three months. Count who came, what it cost, what broke, and what the neighbors said. Run the first public-good morning.

  5. If it holds, look for a standing day room or a garage bay on a plain lease: boards, tools, a table. Still no beds, no purchase.

  6. Only after a year of this does a longer lease or any form of ownership come up, and that starts with a lawyer, the planning counter, and the plain box on the pattern page.

Five questions for the planning counter

  1. Is this address inside the Coastal Zone, and who issues the coastal permit here?
  2. What is this parcel zoned, and is a club, meeting room or lodging use allowed in that zone?
  3. Is a stay under 30 days lawful at this address, and under which permit?
  4. What does the city count as an event or a gathering, and when does it need its own permit?
  5. Which business license and occupancy tax apply, and who has to hold them?
