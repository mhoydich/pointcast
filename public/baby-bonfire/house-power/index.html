        Figures that were opened and read

        • Room light on a small solar cell An indoor amorphous-silicon cell under LED light made about 3 µW per cm² at 200 lux, 10 at 500 lux (normal desk light) and 27 at 1,000 lux. Used for the tile behind a closed curtain (500 lux) and for bright daylight with the sun on the horizon (1,000 lux as a stand-in). A second check the same day could not open the file, so these three figures are not confirmed. University of Southampton ePrints, 2013 conference abstract on solar cells under indoor light · as read Nov 11, 2026
        • Indoor-tuned modules Indoor modules are rated to work between 200 and 1,000 lux. The post's example of an indoor-tuned module is 6% efficient outdoors and can still beat a 22% outdoor module under room light. The 6% is used in the sunny-window sum. PowerFilm blog, Apr 20, 2021, on indoor light harvesting · as read Nov 11, 2026
        • Thermoelectric modules on a stove Eight 40 mm modules on a stove, cooled by fans, made up to 14.79 W with about 148 °C across them, or 1.85 W per module. After running the fans, 8.25 W was left at 5 V. Measured conversion was 2.31%, beside roughly 626 W of heat into the room air. Used for both pucks and for the heating row. MDPI Materials 11(6), 966 (2018), stove-powered thermoelectric generator with fan cooling · as read Nov 11, 2026
        • A thermoelectric module over a candle A candle-heated module gives on the order of fractions of a watt, enough for a few small lights and no more. Used as a sense check: both puck figures here sit at or below that. Open Electronics, practical test of a candle-powered thermoelectric lamp · as read Nov 11, 2026
        • Inline turbine on a tap Small inline water turbines list 5 V or 12 V at up to 150 mA, with 3.5 to 20 litres a minute of flow and 0.05 MPa of pressure to start. The 0.75 W used here is 5 V × 150 mA, a ceiling worked out by a reader in the comments; the article reports no measured watts. CNX Software, Apr 29, 2020, on 5 V and 12 V micro water turbines · as read Nov 11, 2026
        • One-hand cranking The main test subjects held 54 W (give or take 14) at the handle for 30 minutes at 60 turns a minute. The authors put the level most people could hold at 31 W and assume about half of it reaches the battery. Used: 31 W × 50%. The Design Society, paper on comfortable one-hand cranking · as read Nov 11, 2026
        • Footstep tiles, and the small light A review inside this paper lists a piezo floor at 3.49 mJ per step, a commercial paver at 0.3 to 0.6 J per step and an electromagnetic paver at 1.8 J per step. Its own bench light drew 2.5 V × 28 mA, or 0.07 W. Used: 0.3 J (the low end) for the springy tile, 3.49 mJ for the piezo tile, and 0.07 W as the small light everywhere on this page. De Gruyter, Energy Harvesting and Systems (2024), electricity generation through footstep tiles · as read Nov 11, 2026

        Illustrative, not sourced

        • Sun tile size: 5 cm × 5 cm, 25 cm².
        • Sunny window: 100 mW per cm² of full sun, with half lost to glass and angle. One more page on indoor solar was tried and would not open.
        • Fanless pucks: 50 °C across the one on the stove, 15 °C across the one by the hearth, with output falling as the square of that difference.
        • Downspout: a 50 m² roof, 5 mm of rain an hour, a 3 m fall and a turbine that catches 10%. No downspout figure was opened.
        • Door: 5 newtons of extra push over 1.2 m of handle travel, 20% converted.
        • Spending: a puck that averages 50 µW awake, a bell strike of 0.2 J, a tag read of 0.25 W for a tenth of a second, and an evening of 3 hours.
        • Ember bank: holds 1,000 J and starts with 6 J.
        • Big loads: a 1,500 W heater, a 2,000 W cooking ring, a 100 W television.

        Every sum on this page rounds each step to three figures and carries the rounded value forward, so the lines can be checked by hand.
