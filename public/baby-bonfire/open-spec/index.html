Open spec v0

The smallest description of the system that someone else could build against. It is short on purpose.

This is a draft and it is open to change. Nobody has built to it. Nothing in it has been tested, including the fire safety rules. Where a number or a part would normally go, this page lists a question instead.

1. The place

A place is somewhere a piece can land. It can be a palm-size tile, or it can be any spot in a home that someone has marked: a shelf, a sill, the arm of a couch.

  • A place has a tap tag, so a piece and a place can tell which is which when they meet.
  • A place may hold a swappable puck. The puck is the part that senses. The first puck senses heat and light. A later one could sense air.
  • The puck is optional. A place with no puck is still a place: a marked spot where a piece belongs.

2. The piece

A piece is anything with a tag. A small hearth, a candle, a bell, a teapot, a stone from a beach, a toy. If someone loves it and it carries a tag, it is a piece.

The spec does not say what a piece looks like. The style is plural.

3. Signals

A place can say four things, and nothing else.

  • LandedA piece has been set down on this place.
  • LiftedThe piece has been picked up.
  • LitThere is a flame, or the flameless stand-in for one, on this place.
  • SnuffedThe flame has been put out.

There is no signal for who is in the room, what was said, how long someone stayed, or anything else about a person. A longer list would make the system more capable and less trustworthy, so the list stays at four.

4. Rules

  • No cameras and no microphones. Not in a place, a puck or a piece.
  • It works with nothing plugged in. A piece on a place is already the whole thing. Power and a network add to it and are never required.
  • Every networked object has a physical off, and it is off by default. The owner turns the network on, object by object, and can turn it off the same way.
  • Signals are given by a person's act, never taken. Someone sets a piece down or lights a flame. Nothing is inferred about them from the background.
  • “Not lit” and “not connected” must look different. If a linked light in another home is dark, the person looking at it must be able to tell an unlit hearth from a broken link.

5. Fire safety rules

These are rules for anyone designing a piece that holds a real flame. They are a starting list written by people who are not fire safety engineers, and they have not been reviewed by one.

  • Contained. The flame sits inside a vessel that can hold it.
  • Lidded. Every flame piece has a lid, and the lid going on is how the flame goes out.
  • No liquid fuel.
  • Flameless for anyone at risk. Every flame piece needs a flameless version that sends the same signals, for homes where a real flame is the wrong choice.
  • A real flame is never ignited remotely. A network can carry the news that a flame was lit. It can never light one.

Do not burn anything indoors on the strength of this page. A design that follows this list is not thereby safe.

6. What is undecided

  • Dimensions. How big a tile is, how thick, and what footprint a piece has to fit. “Palm-size” is the only measurement so far.
  • The connector. How a puck seats in a place, and how it gets power if it needs any.
  • The radio. Which one, if any, and what the tap tag is. The open mesh notes list what the radio has to allow and do not pick one.

The licence is chosen (Nov 12, 2026): CC BY 4.0 for the spec and the words, CERN-OHL-P v2 for files to print. The licence note says which is which.

If you have a view on any of these, the make a piece page says how to send it.

One proposal for the first two, with files to print, is on the first place page. It is a proposal that nobody has printed yet, and it settles nothing here.
