Other people's work in the directions this project is exploring: labs, design firms, artists, architects, students and a few very old traditions. Every entry was checked on a page that was opened, and every card links out to its owner. The last shelf, Earlier on PointCast, is different: it lists pages already on this site that circle the same ground, each with a line taken from its own block or page.

As of Nov 11, 2026

What the wall says

  1. Plenty of research, few survivors. The Ambient Orb's data network stopped in 2019, Sifteo Cubes and GravityLight were discontinued, Jibo's company sold its assets in 2018, and Tempescope never reached shops. The products still on sale do one humble job: Toniebox, Yoto, the Ecofan (since 1994) and Zojirushi's kettle service (since 2001).

  2. A tile can know what sits on it without a camera. NFC and RFID tags (Toniebox, Mir:ror, Project Zanzibar), electromagnetic tags (musicBottles) and plain weight (Drift Table, History Tablecloth) have all been built and lived with.

  3. Family presence works as a side effect of ordinary acts. Boiling a kettle, touching a picture frame, switching on a lamp. The Digital Family Portrait asked nothing of the parent beyond an ordinary day.

  4. Flame is used for power and for time, almost never as a signal. Stove fans, camp stoves and candle lamps turn heat into electricity; incense clocks and ten-minute incense matches turn burning into a timer. The closest thing found to a lit flame the house responds to is a student pair of candles with LED flames. That ground looks open.

  5. Earth houses and Mars habitats use the same method, and the oldest networks are made of fire. Both print local material in layers; Casa Covida arranges its rooms around a hearth, a bath and the sky. Beacon chains crossed Asia Minor in the 9th century, and Lyon still lights its windows every December 8. El Segundo has its own versions a short walk away.
