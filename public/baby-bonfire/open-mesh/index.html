Open mesh notes

How places could talk to each other, inside one home and between homes, without anyone signing up for anything.

This is exploration. No network exists. No radio or protocol has been picked. These notes say what a network would have to allow, so that whoever picks one has something to check it against.

What travels

Only the four signals in the open spec: landed, lifted, lit, snuffed. Each one says which place and which piece. That is the whole message.

No audio, no images, no location, no message text. If a network can only carry four words, there is very little it can leak.

Inside one home

  • Places talk to each other directly, over a local mesh. The hearth on the shelf can tell the lamp by the door without anything leaving the house.
  • It works with the internet unplugged.
  • There is no hub that has to be bought, and no app that has to be installed for the basics.
  • Any place that is switched off is simply absent. The others carry on.

Between homes

A linked place shows, in one home, a light from another. A hearth lit in one kitchen makes a tile glow in another. A row of them is a beacon chain: one home lights, the next sees it and lights, and so on down the line.

  • A link is made between two specific objects by the two people who own them, with both objects in hand or by passing something from one person to the other. How exactly is an open question.
  • A link carries only lit and snuffed, unless both owners choose more.
  • Either owner can end a link alone, at any time, without the other's agreement.
  • A dark linked light must show whether the far hearth is unlit or the link is down. This is a rule in the spec because the two mean very different things to the person looking.

One way it could look

Mesh Street is a sketch you can play with: an invented town of fourteen houses where lighting a hearth sends a signal from home to home over three layers, short hops between things that are close, small radios on rooftops, and relays on the internet. When no path is open the signal waits as an ember and moves on later.

It is a drawing of one possibility. The ranges in it are approximate, and nothing in it has been picked or built. The signal reports that a hearth was lit. It never lights one.

On or off as the owner decides

  • Opt-in per object. Turning on the network for one place turns it on for that place only.
  • Off by default, with a physical off. A switch, a lid, a puck pulled out. Something a person can see and reach, not a setting.
  • No account. Nothing to register, no company in the middle that has to stay in business for a light to work.
  • Open. Anyone can build a place or a piece that joins, from the published description, without asking permission.
  • Nothing lights a flame. The network reports fire. It never starts one.

The same words elsewhere on PointCast

PointCast already uses “mesh”, “federation” and “beacon”. Those pages are about a website and a town, not about objects in a home, and none of them is a device network. They are related in spirit: small independent things that find each other without a centre.

  • /mesh describes the three networks PointCast itself sits in: a local one within 25 miles of El Segundo, an online one of channels and feeds, and one made of the machine-readable pages that agents read.
  • /federation is a roster of other sites near PointCast, drawn as nodes in rings by how close they are. Some are real and some are marked as imagined.
  • /beacon draws a 25-mile circle around El Segundo and lists the neighbourhoods inside it that the site hopes to reach.
  • /loom is PointCast's public message system, where a message can be appended to any address. A home network of four signals is far smaller than that and would not be public.

If a Baby Bonfire mesh is ever built, the honest link to these pages is the habit they share: publish the description in the open and let other people's things join.

Open questions

  • Which radio, or more than one. It has to work with no internet and no hub.
  • How two homes link without an account and without a company relaying for them.
  • What happens when a home's network is off for a month. Does the far side see “not connected” forever, or does a link expire?
  • How a link is shown to exist, so nobody is surprised to find their hearth lights something elsewhere.
  • Whether anything should be stored at all. The simplest answer is no history: a place knows its state now and nothing before.
