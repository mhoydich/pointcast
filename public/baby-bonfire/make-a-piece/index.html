Make a piece

This exploration is open. Five kinds of thing are welcome, and none of them needs permission first.

The licence is not chosen yet. The files are public to read. Until a licence is picked, what you may do with them, and what others may do with what you send, is not settled. If that matters for your contribution, say so when you send it.

What you can send

  • 1

    A scene

    A single web page someone can play with on a phone, like the twenty-one on the street. One file, styles and script inside it, no outside requests, no tracking.

  • 2

    A piece idea

    An object, what it stands for, and what the room does when it lands or is lit. A paragraph and a drawing are plenty.

  • 3

    A file for a printer or a laser

    A stand, a lid, a tile, a puck seat. Say what machine and material you made it for, and whether you have actually made one. The Home Press is a sketch of a bench such files might run on one day. It is a drawing, and nothing in it has been built. The first place page has the first set of files: a tile and a puck that nobody has printed yet.

  • 4

    A test result

    You built something and tried it. Write down what you did and what happened, including what went wrong. Results that say “this did not work” are as useful as the other kind.

  • 5

    A ritual

    Something people do with fire, water, air, earth, light or sound when a day or a life changes. Say where it comes from. Practices that belong to a specific people are theirs; the Ritual Atlas explains what it leaves alone and why.

Where it goes

Everything on these pages lives in one folder of the PointCast repository on GitHub.

Open the files Open an issue

  • If you use GitHub, open an issue with your idea or a pull request with your file.
  • If you do not, write to hello@pointcast.xyz with “Baby Bonfire” in the subject.
  • Nothing is merged or published without Mike Hoydich's say.

What every contribution keeps to

  • No cameras and no microphones, in an object or in a page.
  • No claim that something is safe, tested or good for anyone unless you tested it and say how.
  • Real flame follows the fire safety rules in the open spec, and always has a flameless version.
  • Other people's work is described in your own words, credited and linked.
  • Nothing is for sale here.
