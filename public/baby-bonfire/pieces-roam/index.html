The Roaming Shelf

Eight pieces, seventeen places, four rooms. Nothing has a fixed spot. Where things sit is what the home hears.

Places sense presence and arrangement only: which piece is on which spot. No cameras, no microphones. This page keeps nothing and sends nothing.

Drag a piece to another spot. Or tap a piece, then tap a spot.

Keyboard: Tab to a piece, Enter to pick it up, Tab or arrow keys to a spot, Enter to set it down. Escape lets go.

What this home hears

  • HomeAny piece on the entry shelf. The entry light comes on.
  • Out todayThe hat on its hook. The hall stays low until the hat comes down. Anything else on the hook is only hanging there.
  • WorkingThe stone on the desk. The desk lamp is on.
  • Workday overThe stone carried from the desk to the window. The desk lamp goes off and the window lamp comes on.
  • DinnerThree pieces gathered on the table. The table light comes up and the other rooms dim.
  • The desk is a shrineAll three desk spots taken. The home leaves the desk exactly as it is.

Why the shelf roams

An earlier sketch, The Shelf, kept every piece on one fixed row of five stones. Mike Hoydich corrected that on Nov 11, 2026:

“all these pieces i move around my home it's part of the ritual”
Mike Hoydich, in chat. He also said a cluttered desk “is a shrine”.

So this version has no shelf. Any object can be a piece: a mug, a stone, a hat, a toy robot, an unlit candle, a jar of red dust that says it is from Mars. The places are spots around the house, and each home decides what an arrangement means. The six meanings above are one invented home's choices.

A place knows one thing: which tagged piece is resting on it. It cannot see, hear or record. The lights in this drawing are the page responding; nothing here controls a real lamp, and nothing here lights a flame. The candle stays unlit.
