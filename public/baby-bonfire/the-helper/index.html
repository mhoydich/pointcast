The Helper

7:10 pm
7:10 pm · evening

Drag the stone or the bell, light the hearth, open the window, or let time pass. The helper answers with a small chore and says which place sensed what.

Baby Bonfire · concept sketch · Nov 2026

What it can't do

These are this sketch's assumptions about a helper the size of a teapot. None of it is built. It has no camera, no microphone and no voice: it knows what the pucks read and which tags are near its reader.

Stairs
It lives on one floor. It stops at the bottom step and at any ledge taller than a doormat. Drop a piece on the stairs here and watch it give up.
Anything hot
It will not touch or carry what a heat puck reads as warm, so it waits for cool before it trims a wick. The snuffer is the one tool it brings near a flame: a cup on a long handle.
Lighting a flame
It never lights one, and nothing in the house can. A person strikes the match. The helper can only end a flame.
Anything it wasn't shown
It knows the pieces and places someone tapped it against, and it finds its way by those tiles. An object it was never shown is furniture: it steers around it. Move a tile and it has to be shown again.
High or heavy
It reaches about a hand above the floor and lifts about what a full mug weighs. Counters, sofas, beds and the plant pot itself are out of reach, so every place here sits low.
Knowing who is there
It cannot tell who is in a room. It only knows a key left the door dish, or a tile went empty. A piece that no place misses is not lost to it.
Doors, windows, taps
It cannot open or shut them, and it cannot refill its own water flask. One measure per dry reading is all it gives.
Standing in for a person near fire
It is not a safety device. It ends a flame only when it has charge, a clear floor and the snuffer in reach. Nobody should leave a flame for it.
