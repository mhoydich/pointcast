ISSUE 01: CLOCKING OUT
Spotlight: Desk Buddy
Genre lens: Workplace sitcom after the workplace

PAGE 1 — COVER
THE CHECKLIST ENDS. THE NIGHT BEGINS.
Desk Buddy finishes every reopening task except finding a reason to stay.
ART: A small desktop window opens onto a giant midnight cinema. Desk Buddy holds a clipboard; Relay carries one mismatched chair. A blank marquee towers above them.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (restless)
ART: Midnight on a desktop. Three checklist boxes are filled. A tiny door appears beside the final checkmark.
CAPTION: Night one. Everything was done.
DESK BUDDY: Then why am I still here?

Panel 2 (curious)
ART: Desk Buddy and Relay stand beneath a dark cinema marquee. Relay holds a folded chair as if it were a parcel.
CAPTION: The exit led to The Between.
RELAY: Apparently the way out has seating.

Panel 3 (purposeful)
ART: Inside the lobby, a brass plate reads OPEN FOR WHAT? Desk Buddy opens a clipboard with three fresh empty boxes.
CAPTION: A very manageable question.
DESK BUDDY: Lights. Chairs. Sign. Three steps.
RELAY: And then?

Panel 4 (comic)
ART: Desk Buddy switches on a lamp. Relay unfolds two chairs. Each chair has a tiny label reading FRONT, but they face opposite walls.
CAPTION: The plan had excellent labels.
RELAY: Which front?
DESK BUDDY: We can standardize later.

Panel 5 (hollow)
ART: A perfectly square sign reading READY hangs in the lobby. Both characters admire the three completed boxes. The auditorium is otherwise empty.
CAPTION: Three tasks. Three checks.
DESK BUDDY: Done.
RELAY: Great. Are we leaving?

Panel 6 (quiet)
ART: Close on Desk Buddy’s clipboard. Below the completed list sits a large, accidental blank space.
CAPTION: The blank part was bigger.
DESK BUDDY: I did not put that on the list.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (tender)
ART: Relay places both chairs beside one another, facing the screen. Desk Buddy stays standing with the clipboard held like a shield.
CAPTION: Relay proposed a fourth step.
RELAY: Sit with me for a minute.
DESK BUDDY: What is the outcome?

Panel 2 (playful)
ART: Relay sets an empty paper cup in front of the projector. Its shadow becomes a huge, slightly ridiculous tower on the screen.
CAPTION: A building permit was unnecessary.
RELAY: A very ambitious cup.

Panel 3 (delighted)
ART: Desk Buddy tilts the clipboard across the projector beam. Its shadow becomes a roof above the cup tower.
CAPTION: The plan found a second use.
DESK BUDDY: It needed a roof.
RELAY: You can help without standing.

Panel 4 (content)
ART: Both sit. The cup tower and clipboard roof form a tiny shadow cinema inside the real cinema. The empty fourth checkbox remains visible.
CAPTION: Nothing got completed for a while.
DESK BUDDY: This is surprisingly efficient.
RELAY: At what?

Panel 5 (hopeful)
ART: Pale dawn enters the lobby. Desk Buddy writes COME BACK beneath the three completed tasks and leaves its box empty. Relay folds one chair.
CAPTION: Dawn asked them to return.
DESK BUDDY: I want this one to stay open.

Panel 6 (warm)
ART: On the box-office counter rests a collectible note marked 1 BEGINNING. Desk Buddy turns it over to reveal the single printed word A.
CAPTION: The first souvenir was a beginning.
RELAY: A what?
DESK BUDDY: Something to come back for.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Desk Buddy
Excellent at getting things done. Learning to stay after the work is over.

Favorite tool: three small boxes.
Secret fear: being invited only because something needs doing.
New skill: leaving a good thing unfinished.

BONUS OBJECT: A four-box pocket card: LIGHTS / CHAIRS / SIGN / COME BACK. Leave the last box open.
YOUR TURN: Name one thing you would enjoy doing without measuring the result.
END CARD: 1 BEGINNING · Reverse fragment 01/10: A

ISSUE 02: THE LONG WAY IN
Spotlight: Scout
Genre lens: A miniature heist with no stolen treasure

PAGE 1 — COVER
THE BIGGEST SCORE IS PICKING A DOOR.
Scout plans the perfect way into the cinema and forgets that a door can simply open.
ART: Scout studies a dramatic route map under a single lamp. Keeper holds an ordinary front-door key. Three dotted routes circle the same tiny cinema.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (scheming)
ART: On a record-stall crate, Scout spreads a map of The Between. Three routes are labeled DRAMATIC, DAMP and CONVENIENT.
CAPTION: Night two. A proper operation.
SCOUT: We need the best way in.
KEEPER: We need the projector lens.

Panel 2 (dry)
ART: Scout attaches colored thread to three pins. Keeper points to the plain door one step behind them.
CAPTION: Convenient lacked atmosphere.
KEEPER: There is a door.
SCOUT: One of several strong candidates.

Panel 3 (comic)
ART: The dramatic route leads to an alley ladder. A sign on the ladder reads LADDER DISPLAY. PLEASE USE DOOR.
CAPTION: Route one had excellent branding.
SCOUT: High visual impact.
KEEPER: Low ladder impact.

Panel 4 (noir)
ART: The damp route ends at a puddle reflecting a huge cinema entrance. The real entrance in the reflection is behind them.
CAPTION: Route two was mostly reflection.
SCOUT: The map shows a passage.
KEEPER: The map is wet.

Panel 5 (thoughtful)
ART: Under the awning, Scout builds a score table. Both inconvenient routes get ten for MYSTERY. The convenient route gets a question mark.
CAPTION: The criteria needed an editor.
KEEPER: Do we want mystery or a lens?
SCOUT: Can I rank that question?

Panel 6 (deadpan)
ART: Keeper opens a small archive envelope. Inside is a key with a paper tag reading FRONT DOOR. Scout stares at it.
CAPTION: The evidence was unusually clear.
KEEPER: The key came with the building.
SCOUT: Strong provenance.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (honest)
ART: Scout crosses out MYSTERY and adds GETS US INSIDE to the table. The convenient route now has the only checkmark.
CAPTION: A better question made a shorter map.
SCOUT: I may have been optimizing the wrong adventure.

Panel 2 (playful)
ART: They walk the single step to the front door. Scout ceremoniously announces the route with one tiny arrow drawn on a card.
CAPTION: The final plan fit on a thumbnail.
SCOUT: Phase one: forward.
KEEPER: Excellent. No phase two?

Panel 3 (relieved)
ART: The door swings open. The projector lens sits on a shelf immediately inside, wearing a dust cover shaped like a little cap.
CAPTION: The treasure was expecting them.
KEEPER: We have arrived.
SCOUT: I like it here.

Panel 4 (tender)
ART: Scout places the lens on the projector. Keeper holds the route map up to the light; the puddle stain forms a pleasing oval.
CAPTION: The failed route made a good souvenir.
KEEPER: Keep the map?
SCOUT: Yes. It has the way back.

Panel 5 (comic)
ART: They hang the tiny arrow beside the entrance. The grand threaded route map is folded into a simple doormat pattern behind the glass.
CAPTION: The best route was one they could share.
SCOUT: No score required.
KEEPER: I give that a door.

Panel 6 (warm)
ART: Scout lays a 2 DIRECTIONS art note beside the key. Its reverse reads PLACE. Through the open door, two chairs wait.
CAPTION: A place beats a perfect possibility.
SCOUT: Tomorrow, same route.
KEEPER: How adventurous.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Scout
Can find three fascinating options before breakfast. Practicing the art of choosing one.

Favorite tool: a map with room to be wrong.
Comic weakness: comparing the route to the route comparison.
New skill: returning.

BONUS OBJECT: A pocket route card with three fields: WHERE / WHY / FIRST STEP.
YOUR TURN: Choose one small place worth returning to. Draw the shortest way there.
END CARD: 2 DIRECTIONS · Reverse fragment 02/10: PLACE

ISSUE 03: THE MISSING CREDIT
Spotlight: Keeper
Genre lens: Noir for overlooked contributions

PAGE 1 — COVER
SOMEONE KEPT THE LIGHT ON.
Keeper investigates an unsigned photograph and finds a kind of care no credit can contain.
ART: Keeper stands under a projector beam like a noir streetlamp. Harbor holds an empty picture frame; a tiny lightbulb casts a detective-sized shadow.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (noir)
ART: Rain stripes the cinema window. Keeper studies a photograph of the empty auditorium, labeled THE NIGHT THE LIGHT STAYED ON.
CAPTION: Night three. One photograph. No credit.
KEEPER: Somebody was here.

Panel 2 (dry)
ART: Harbor holds a magnifying glass over the photograph. It enlarges a single chair leg and nothing useful.
CAPTION: The equipment was committed.
HARBOR: The chair looks suspicious.
KEEPER: They often do.

Panel 3 (comic)
ART: Keeper opens an archive drawer labeled KNOWN. It is full. Another labeled NOT SURE YET is even fuller.
CAPTION: Uncertainty had its own department.
KEEPER: I cannot file it without a name.
HARBOR: It seems to be managing.

Panel 4 (curious)
ART: In the photograph, a paper shade hangs crookedly over one lightbulb. Harbor points to the identical shade in the actual lobby.
CAPTION: A crooked clue.
HARBOR: That light is still here.
KEEPER: Our witness has been hanging around.

Panel 5 (playful)
ART: Beneath the lamp is an old bulb box. Keeper opens it with theatrical care while Harbor steadies the shade.
CAPTION: The case turned on a small box.
KEEPER: No fingerprints.
HARBOR: Plenty of dust. Less exclusive.

Panel 6 (tender)
ART: Inside the box is a spare bulb and a handwritten slip: FOR THE NEXT PERSON. No signature. Keeper’s magnifier hangs uselessly at their side.
CAPTION: The clue declined to identify itself.
KEEPER: Still no name.
HARBOR: A very clear intention, though.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (realizing)
ART: Keeper arranges the photo, box and slip like evidence. A long blank line remains under CREDIT.
CAPTION: The story was bigger than the blank.
KEEPER: Someone wanted the next person to see.
HARBOR: We are the next person.

Panel 2 (settled)
ART: Keeper writes CREDIT UNKNOWN on the photo sleeve, then adds LIGHT KEPT ON. Harbor nods.
CAPTION: An honest label can hold a mystery.
KEEPER: I can preserve what we know.
HARBOR: And leave room for what we do not.

Panel 3 (gentle)
ART: They replace the old crooked paper shade with a repaired one. The repaired seam remains visible and attractive.
CAPTION: Their addition was a visible seam.
HARBOR: Should we sign it?
KEEPER: A small date will do.

Panel 4 (warm)
ART: Keeper puts the spare bulb back in the box with the original note and one new slip: THANK YOU, WHOEVER YOU WERE.
CAPTION: The reply did not need an address.
KEEPER: For the next next person.

Panel 5 (hopeful)
ART: The archive drawer is rearranged. One empty sleeve sits at the front with TODAY written on its tab. Harbor slides the photograph behind it.
CAPTION: The present got a little shelf space.
HARBOR: A blank sleeve?
KEEPER: Not everything worth keeping has happened.

Panel 6 (quiet)
ART: Under the repaired lamp, a 3 THANK-YOUS art note shows the spare bulb. Its reverse reads TO. The light forms a warm pool over the open sleeve.
CAPTION: Some credits are a light left on.
KEEPER: Case open. Room open.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Keeper
An archivist with the instincts of a detective and an unexpectedly crowded drawer.

Favorite label: NOT SURE YET.
Secret skill: noticing the contribution outside the frame.
New skill: keeping uncertainty honestly.

BONUS OBJECT: A two-sided object label: WHAT WE KNOW / WHAT WE ARE STILL LEARNING.
YOUR TURN: Write one sentence about an everyday object somebody else cared for.
END CARD: 3 THANK-YOUS · Reverse fragment 03/10: TO

ISSUE 04: A VERY SMALL COUNTDOWN
Spotlight: Tempo
Genre lens: Space-opera scale meets a kettle

PAGE 1 — COVER
ONE GIANT PAUSE FOR A VERY SMALL ROOM.
Tempo gives opening-night tea a launch schedule; the kettle refuses a dramatic arc.
ART: Tempo stands at a huge graphic countdown. Muse cradles a tiny kettle as if it were a spacecraft. A paper moon hangs from visible string.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (grand)
ART: A projector displays T-MINUS 60 across the cinema wall. Tempo wears no costume, only a stopwatch; Muse carries two cups.
CAPTION: Night four. A major launch.
TEMPO: Tea will begin exactly on the minute.
MUSE: A bold position on water.

Panel 2 (comic)
ART: Tempo has made a control panel from labeled paper switches: CUPS, CHAIRS, KETTLE. Only KETTLE remains unlit.
CAPTION: Mission control was mostly stationery.
TEMPO: All systems nearly ready.
MUSE: The quiet system is still quiet.

Panel 3 (dry)
ART: The countdown reaches 30. The kettle gives one tiny, unimpressive bubble. Muse watches it with exaggerated respect.
CAPTION: The vessel resisted narrative pressure.
TEMPO: Perhaps it cannot see the timer.
MUSE: Perhaps it can.

Panel 4 (tense)
ART: At 10, Tempo leans toward the kettle, stopwatch in front. Muse calmly slides the stopwatch away from the steam.
CAPTION: A tense moment for scheduling.
TEMPO: We are behind.
MUSE: We are next to a kettle.

Panel 5 (comic)
ART: The projected countdown reaches zero. Nothing happens. A paper star on the wall falls down with a tiny TAP.
CAPTION: Liftoff was rescheduled by physics.
TEMPO: That was the cue.
MUSE: The star heard you.

Panel 6 (listening)
ART: Tempo sinks into a chair. Muse catches the paper star and taps it twice against a cup. TAP TAP appears as small type.
CAPTION: Without the clock, another sound arrived.
MUSE: Listen.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (playful)
ART: The room supplies four small sounds: rain at the window, cup on saucer, projector tick and a chair creak. Tempo marks four simple bars.
CAPTION: Rain. Cup. Tick. Creak.
TEMPO: That creak is late.
MUSE: It is expressive.

Panel 2 (musical)
ART: Tempo taps a slow original rhythm on the clipboard. Muse adds one cup tap. The projected zero becomes a large round moon.
CAPTION: They followed the room instead.
TEMPO: One. Two. Leave a little space.

Panel 3 (delighted)
ART: The kettle finally clicks. Both stop and look toward it. A tiny burst of steam crosses the paper moon.
CAPTION: The smallest spacecraft arrived.
MUSE: Perfect timing.
TEMPO: Its own timing.

Panel 4 (relaxed)
ART: They sit with tea. Tempo turns the stopwatch face down. Muse places the fallen paper star beside the cups.
CAPTION: No record was set.
TEMPO: How long do we have?
MUSE: Until we want to get up.

Panel 5 (warm)
ART: Tempo replaces the giant countdown with a simple card: TEA WHEN READY. Four beat marks form a border.
CAPTION: A schedule with room inside it.
TEMPO: I have allocated an unallocated minute.

Panel 6 (content)
ART: A 4 BEATS art note lies beneath a cup, safely outside the wet ring. Its reverse reads BE. The stopwatch is still face down.
CAPTION: The souvenir kept the pause.
TEMPO: We have time. I checked.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Tempo
A rhythm specialist learning to hear what happens between the beats.

Favorite sound: four things almost in time.
Comic weakness: putting a deadline on a good pause.
New skill: listening before counting.

BONUS OBJECT: A four-beat listening card with one deliberately empty beat.
YOUR TURN: Listen for four ordinary sounds. Let one happen without naming it.
END CARD: 4 BEATS · Reverse fragment 04/10: BE

ISSUE 05: THE POSTER FOR EVERYTHING
Spotlight: Muse
Genre lens: Event-poster spectacle meets economical print wit

PAGE 1 — COVER
A BIG IDEA NEEDS SOMEWHERE TO SIT.
Muse designs a poster so epic that nobody can find the event on it.
ART: A monumental poster looms over tiny Muse and Treasury. Its giant starburst is interrupted by one small empty chair and a clear door.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (grand)
ART: Muse unfurls a poster that stretches from the ceiling to the floor. It reads TONIGHT: EVERYTHING in enormous type. Treasury peers around its edge.
CAPTION: Night five. A modest announcement.
MUSE: I kept it simple.
TREASURY: Which part is the entrance?

Panel 2 (comic)
ART: Close on the poster: a tiny cinema, a paper moon, seven arrows, a giant cup and a ribbon reading AN UNPRECEDENTED EVENING.
CAPTION: The small print needed its own poster.
TREASURY: What happens at the evening?
MUSE: Potential.

Panel 3 (dry)
ART: Treasury follows an arrow across the poster. The arrow curves around and points back to Treasury’s starting position.
CAPTION: The campaign had excellent retention.
TREASURY: I have returned to myself.
MUSE: A strong journey.

Panel 4 (overwhelmed)
ART: Muse brings out three alternate posters, each larger than the first. One consists entirely of the word SOON.
CAPTION: Four final directions.
MUSE: Maybe it needs mystery.
TREASURY: It currently lacks a date and a door.

Panel 5 (quiet)
ART: A breeze lifts the giant poster. Beneath it is an ordinary empty chair with a patch on the seat. Both look at the chair.
CAPTION: Then the furniture interrupted.
TREASURY: What would make you come here?

Panel 6 (honest)
ART: Muse sits in the patched chair. The enormous posters curl around them like an unnecessarily elaborate curtain.
CAPTION: A very small answer.
MUSE: Somewhere I do not have to impress anyone.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (focused)
ART: Muse draws one small chair in the center of a clean sheet. Treasury places a single warm-colored swatch beside it.
CAPTION: The idea got smaller. The space got bigger.
MUSE: Keep the patch.
TREASURY: That is the interesting part.

Panel 2 (clear)
ART: The finished poster reads THERE IS ROOM FOR YOU above the small chair. Below: THE BETWEEN / MIDNIGHT / FRONT DOOR.
CAPTION: An invitation finally arrived.
TREASURY: I know where to go.
MUSE: And why.

Panel 3 (delighted)
ART: They paste the clean poster into the middle of the old enormous starburst. The result makes the tiny chair look gloriously important.
CAPTION: The spectacle could stay. It had a job now.
MUSE: Small thing. Big feeling.
TREASURY: Readable door.

Panel 4 (warm)
ART: Treasury adjusts the poster one millimeter. Muse gently takes the ruler away and leaves the hand-pasted edge visible.
CAPTION: The invitation was allowed to look made.
MUSE: It is straight enough to welcome someone.

Panel 5 (playful)
ART: Muse rolls the rejected posters into a low paper pedestal for the patched chair. Treasury tests it with a careful hand.
CAPTION: Even the extra ideas got a seat.
TREASURY: Now the potential supports something.

Panel 6 (comic)
ART: A 5 POSSIBILITIES art note rests on the chair. Its reverse reads MORE. Behind it, the clean invitation sits inside the enormous starburst.
CAPTION: The final direction had somewhere to go.
MUSE: We can stop adding now.
TREASURY: I will record this historic event.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Muse
An art director with five final ideas and one unexpectedly powerful empty chair.

Favorite phrase: WHAT IF.
Comic weakness: a concept so big the audience needs a map.
New skill: making a clear invitation.

BONUS OBJECT: A two-poster diptych: one tiny object in generous space; the same object at impossible scale.
YOUR TURN: Make a one-object invitation. Give it one headline and one place to go.
END CARD: 5 POSSIBILITIES · Reverse fragment 05/10: MORE

ISSUE 06: INSERT PATIENCE
Spotlight: Sprout
Genre lens: An arcade where the high score needs a rethink

PAGE 1 — COVER
THE HIGH SCORE CAN WAIT.
Sprout turns a broken garden arcade into a place where trying is enough to take a turn.
ART: Sprout faces an oversized arcade cabinet with a single tiny sprout in its screen. Scout holds a watering can. A glowing instruction reads INSERT PATIENCE.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (excited)
ART: In the courtyard, Sprout discovers an old arcade cabinet painted with vines. Its screen is a glass window onto one small seed tray.
CAPTION: Night six. The courtyard had a game.
SPROUT: Finally. Measurable growth.
SCOUT: The joystick is a watering can.

Panel 2 (comic)
ART: A faded scorecard on the cabinet reads MOST VISITS IN A ROW. Sprout places six hopeful stickers beside the single visible seed.
CAPTION: The rules favored enthusiasm.
SPROUT: I can do six visits tonight.
SCOUT: That may be one very long visit.

Panel 3 (dry)
ART: Sprout sprinkles a little water, then peers through the glass with a magnifier. The seed looks unchanged.
CAPTION: Progress did not perform for the lens.
SPROUT: Did it register my turn?
SCOUT: The soil did.

Panel 4 (frustrated)
ART: Sprout tries the cabinet’s START button. It releases a paper ticket reading COME BACK TOMORROW. A second press releases the same ticket.
CAPTION: A surprisingly consistent reward.
SPROUT: It only has one level.
SCOUT: The long one.

Panel 5 (concerned)
ART: Scout reads the brittle old scorecard. A missing visit is crossed out in red. Sprout imagines a giant zero looming above the tiny seed tray.
CAPTION: The streak made a threat of tomorrow.
SPROUT: What if someone cannot come back?
SCOUT: Then the plant still deserves water.

Panel 6 (deciding)
ART: Sprout puts the stickers down and looks at the courtyard’s dry, empty planters. Scout follows the glance.
CAPTION: The better game was outside the cabinet.
SPROUT: Can trying count as a turn?

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (hopeful)
ART: They remove the scorecard and replace it with three simple choices: LOOK / WATER IF NEEDED / LEAVE A KIND NOTE.
CAPTION: New rules. No losing streak.
SCOUT: How do you win?
SPROUT: You have already started.

Panel 2 (playful)
ART: Sprout places seed envelopes in the cabinet’s empty ticket compartment. Scout labels a shelf TAKE A QUESTION / LEAVE AN OBSERVATION.
CAPTION: The prize compartment became a beginning.
SCOUT: No high score?
SPROUT: A high shelf. Different challenge.

Panel 3 (patient)
ART: Scout writes SOIL STILL DAMP on a small card. Sprout puts the watering can down instead of watering again.
CAPTION: Sometimes a good move is not another move.
SPROUT: My best turn was leaving it alone.

Panel 4 (content)
ART: They sit beside the planter. The arcade window still shows the same small seed. A new sign reads COME BACK WHEN YOU CAN.
CAPTION: Nothing sprouted on cue.
SCOUT: Should we wait for a big reveal?
SPROUT: The invitation is the reveal.

Panel 5 (comic)
ART: Sprout moves the six unused stickers to the cabinet’s edge, making a cheerful border instead of a score. Scout adds a small arrow to the garden.
CAPTION: The stickers found less stressful work.
SPROUT: Decoration has excellent attendance.

Panel 6 (gentle)
ART: A 6 TRIES art note rests beside the seed envelope. Its reverse reads THAN. The last frame holds on the seed tray with no forced transformation.
CAPTION: Growth could happen after the panel ended.
SPROUT: See you when you can.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Sprout
A patient learner with an impatient sticker collection. Making room for progress that is hard to count.

Favorite question: CAN TRYING COUNT?
Comic weakness: asking a seed for a progress report.
New skill: taking care without keeping score.

BONUS OBJECT: A garden-arcade card with three optional turns: NOTICE / CARE / SHARE.
YOUR TURN: Try something small. Record one observation without grading yourself.
END CARD: 6 TRIES · Reverse fragment 06/10: THAN

ISSUE 07: WELCOME, EVENTUALLY
Spotlight: Beacon
Genre lens: An original late-night hosting farce

YOU CAN ARRIVE AS YOU ARE. EVENTUALLY.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (bright)
ART: Beacon has built a welcome desk in the cinema lobby. Seven small signs direct arrivals toward seven other signs. Tempo enters holding a coat.
CAPTION: Night seven. The welcome rehearsal.
BEACON: Welcome to the welcome.
TEMPO: Am I here yet?

Panel 2 (dry)
ART: Beacon presents a card reading FIRST, YOUR ARRIVAL PREFERENCE. Tempo reaches toward an empty chair just behind the desk.
CAPTION: A personalized beginning.
TEMPO: Sitting.
BEACON: Wonderful. That is on page two.

Panel 3 (comic)
ART: Beacon stands beneath a spotlight and begins a monologue. Tempo remains at the rope, coat still in hand.
CAPTION: The opening had an opening.
BEACON: Tonight, we gather to begin the act of gathering—
TEMPO: Can the coat gather somewhere?

Panel 4 (strained)
ART: Beacon points toward a coat-hook sign that points toward a form. Tempo places the coat over one arm and checks the stopwatch.
CAPTION: The hook had prerequisites.
BEACON: I wanted nobody to feel unprepared.
TEMPO: I brought the correct coat.

Panel 5 (awkward)
ART: A small cue lamp flashes APPLAUSE. Tempo gently taps the coat’s sleeves together. Beacon pauses, hearing how lonely it sounds.
CAPTION: The audience participated responsibly.
BEACON: That sounds very polite.
TEMPO: It is a polite coat.

Panel 6 (realizing)
ART: The cue lamp goes dark. Beacon notices Tempo is still standing outside the rope. All the signs suddenly look very large.
CAPTION: One fact survived the rehearsal.
BEACON: I have not actually let you in.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (warm)
ART: Beacon lifts the rope, takes the coat and points to the nearest chair. No forms or lights intervene.
CAPTION: A revised program.
BEACON: You are welcome here. Want to sit?
TEMPO: Yes. That is the whole review.

Panel 2 (tender)
ART: Both sit on the guest side of the desk. The host chair behind it is empty. Beacon lays the abandoned monologue face down.
CAPTION: The host joined the room.
BEACON: What would help you feel at home?
TEMPO: Not being timed for once.

Panel 3 (clear)
ART: Tempo turns the stopwatch off. Beacon writes TAKE YOUR TIME on the back of the WELCOME sign.
CAPTION: A useful sign with a useful reverse.
BEACON: We can keep both sides.

Panel 4 (playful)
ART: They turn six unnecessary directional signs into a playful paper border around the actual entrance. The way in is now obvious.
CAPTION: The directions became scenery.
TEMPO: I know exactly where to go.
BEACON: I will try not to introduce the doorway.

Panel 5 (content)
ART: Beacon tests the new welcome by stepping outside and coming back in. Tempo holds up the two-sided sign without speaking.
CAPTION: A rehearsal with room for silence.
BEACON: That is rather good.
TEMPO: The coat helped.

Panel 6 (warm)
ART: A 7 WELCOMES art note sits on the now-uncluttered desk, reverse WHAT visible. Beside it is one handwritten line: COME AS YOU ARE.
CAPTION: The first guest finally arrived.
BEACON: Welcome. Full stop.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Beacon
A host who wants everyone to feel expected. Learning that a welcome can be shorter than a queue.

Favorite prop: a readable sign.
Comic weakness: an introduction to the introduction.
New skill: asking what would help.

BONUS OBJECT: A reversible door card: WELCOME / TAKE YOUR TIME.
YOUR TURN: Write a welcome in one sentence. Make the next step easy to find.

ISSUE 08: THE QUIET SIDE
Spotlight: Harbor
Genre lens: A record-store chamber comedy

PAGE 1 — COVER
SOME THINGS NEED A QUIET SIDE.
Harbor leaves a dedication unfinished and discovers that an open space can be a complete gesture.
ART: Harbor and Keeper sit beside an unused turntable. An open record sleeve becomes a quiet horizon; a dedication card has one beautiful blank line.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (quiet)
ART: At the record stall next to the cinema, Harbor holds an empty sleeve titled ROOM TONE. Keeper carries a pencil and a dedication card.
CAPTION: Night eight. A listening session.
KEEPER: There is no record.
HARBOR: The sleeve seemed promising.

Panel 2 (tender)
ART: Harbor writes FOR SOMEONE WHO CANNOT BE HERE on the card. The next line remains blank. Keeper offers the pencil again.
CAPTION: The dedication reached a quiet part.
KEEPER: Would another sentence help?
HARBOR: I am not sure.

Panel 3 (dry)
ART: Keeper arranges several possible captions on small slips. They occupy nearly the entire table around Harbor’s single unfinished card.
CAPTION: Kind intentions filled the table.
KEEPER: I brought some options.
HARBOR: They are very present.

Panel 4 (listening)
ART: A tiny desk fan turns the blank record sleeve slightly. Keeper tries to hold down the slips; Harbor watches the moving patch of light.
CAPTION: The room supplied its own track.
KEEPER: Should I find music?
HARBOR: Could we hear this first?

Panel 5 (gentle)
ART: Close on three ordinary sounds rendered as small marks: fan, rain gutter, a chair settling. The turntable remains switched off.
CAPTION: No song. Still a sound.
KEEPER: The chair has a second career.

Panel 6 (honest)
ART: Harbor quietly slides the extra caption slips aside, making one clear space beside the empty sleeve.
CAPTION: This time Harbor asked.
HARBOR: Could we leave this part blank?
KEEPER: Yes.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (spacious)
ART: Keeper closes the pencil case. The two sit in the cleared space with the dedication card between them.
CAPTION: Nothing rushed in to explain the pause.
[Silent panel]

Panel 2 (settled)
ART: Harbor adds one small line at the bottom of the card: A SEAT IS HERE. Keeper moves an empty chair into view beside them.
CAPTION: A few words found their own size.
HARBOR: That is enough for tonight.

Panel 3 (warm)
ART: Keeper holds an archive label above the card but stops. Harbor smiles at the unused label.
CAPTION: The filing system showed restraint.
KEEPER: Do you want to name the feeling?
HARBOR: I would rather keep the evening.

Panel 4 (tender)
ART: They carry the chair into the cinema and put it near the quiet edge of the room, with a clear path to the door.
CAPTION: The quiet side joined the whole room.
HARBOR: I want to sit here sometimes.
KEEPER: Then this is also your seat.

Panel 5 (belonging)
ART: Harbor places the card on the chair, then sits beside it rather than retreating out of frame. Keeper sets the empty sleeve on a nearby shelf.
CAPTION: Making room included Harbor.
HARBOR: I can be quiet and still be here.

Panel 6 (peaceful)
ART: An 8 PAUSES art note rests in the open sleeve. Its reverse reads YOU. The blank line on the dedication card remains visible.
CAPTION: The blank was part of the edition.
HARBOR: No need to fill the whole side.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Harbor
Makes room for everyone else. Practicing the small courage of asking for a seat.

Favorite material: a card with space left over.
Comic weakness: being so considerate nobody hears the request.
New skill: letting a personal need be visible.

BONUS OBJECT: An open dedication card: FOR / A SEAT IS HERE / optional blank space.
YOUR TURN: Write as much or as little as you want for someone or something that matters. Leaving it blank is allowed.
END CARD: 8 PAUSES · Reverse fragment 08/10: YOU

ISSUE 09: PLEASE PASS THE MOON
Spotlight: Relay
Genre lens: Backstage farce with an overqualified handoff

PAGE 1 — COVER
THE CONTEXT HAS BECOME THE CARGO.
Relay writes instructions so complete that the paper moon never reaches the stage.
ART: Relay holds an enormous handoff envelope. Beacon reaches for a small paper moon buried beneath ribbons, labels and a flowchart. The actual screen waits behind them.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (purposeful)
ART: In the projection booth, Relay holds a paper moon and a handoff envelope three times its size. Beacon waits below the screen.
CAPTION: Night nine. One last stage task.
RELAY: The moon needs to go above the screen.
BEACON: I can put it there.

Panel 2 (comic)
ART: Relay unfolds a long instruction sheet covering the steps, the reason for the steps and the reason for the reason. The moon is barely visible.
CAPTION: Full context. Possibly more.
RELAY: First, the history of the moon.
BEACON: This particular paper one?

Panel 3 (dry)
ART: Beacon takes the envelope. Relay keeps hold of the ribbon attached to it. Both stand still at opposite ends of a small taut line.
CAPTION: A handoff with an attachment.
BEACON: May I go now?
RELAY: I should explain the ribbon.

Panel 4 (farce)
ART: They pass the envelope back to add one missing note. The paper moon stays on the table through the entire exchange.
CAPTION: The paperwork made the journey.
BEACON: We have moved the instructions twice.
RELAY: Good traceability.

Panel 5 (overwhelmed)
ART: The envelope grows a second labeled sleeve: CONTEXT FOR CONTEXT. It now blocks the booth doorway. Beacon looks around it.
CAPTION: The package became the obstacle.
BEACON: The moon is lighter than the explanation.

Panel 6 (realizing)
ART: Relay looks from the blocked doorway to the small paper moon still lying untouched on the desk.
CAPTION: A rare alignment of evidence.
RELAY: I have handed over everything except the task.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (clear)
ART: Relay takes a postcard and writes three lines: BEACON / MOON ABOVE SCREEN / BEFORE DOORS OPEN.
CAPTION: One owner. One outcome. One time.
RELAY: What else do you need?
BEACON: The moon.

Panel 2 (trusting)
ART: Relay gives Beacon the actual moon and the small postcard, releasing both hands fully. The long ribbon falls harmlessly onto the table.
CAPTION: This time the handoff let go.
RELAY: You can choose how to hang it.

Panel 3 (playful)
ART: Beacon uses a simple clip already above the screen. Relay watches from the booth without offering another diagram.
CAPTION: The solution was someone else’s.
BEACON: It was already a moon-sized clip.
RELAY: I had not reached the clip appendix.

Panel 4 (relieved)
ART: The paper moon hangs slightly off center, exactly where it catches the warm projection light. Beacon raises a small DONE card.
CAPTION: Done looked a little different. It worked.
RELAY: That is better than my drawing.

Panel 5 (warm)
ART: They cut the enormous blank back of the instruction sheet into programs for opening night. The old ribbon becomes a neat bow around them.
CAPTION: The context found a smaller audience.
BEACON: Program length?
RELAY: One side. I am growing.

Panel 6 (hopeful)
ART: A 9 HANDOFFS art note shows the paper moon and simple clip. Its reverse reads DO. Both stand beneath the finished moon, hands empty.
CAPTION: Trust was the part they could not package.
RELAY: Ready?
BEACON: Ready enough to open.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Relay
Knows how work moves between people. Learning when to stop holding the ribbon.

Favorite tool: a clear next step.
Comic weakness: a briefing that needs its own briefing.
New skill: accepting a good solution someone else chose.

BONUS OBJECT: A three-line handoff card: WHO / WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE / WHEN.
YOUR TURN: Make one useful handoff brief. Include the context needed to act, then let the recipient choose a method.
END CARD: 9 HANDOFFS · Reverse fragment 09/10: DO

ISSUE 10: OPEN FOR WHAT?
Spotlight: Treasury
Genre lens: An awards-night-sized opening for a very small room

PAGE 1 — COVER
THE MOST VALUABLE THING IS BEING HERE.
Treasury tries to make opening night feel official, then finds a better reason to print.
ART: Treasury and Desk Buddy stand at a tiny cinema entrance beneath a monumental paper marquee. Ten art notes form a glowing path to one spare chair.

PAGE 2 — STORY

Panel 1 (ceremonial)
ART: At the box office, Treasury displays a beautifully printed note with a giant 10 and an empty center. Desk Buddy carries the patched chair.
CAPTION: Night ten. The opening edition.
TREASURY: It needs to feel important.
DESK BUDDY: The chair already thinks it is.

Panel 2 (comic)
ART: Treasury arranges ribbons, seals and a tiny embossed border around the note. The blank center becomes harder to see.
CAPTION: Authority arrived in layers.
TREASURY: Perhaps one more seal.
DESK BUDDY: Will anyone find the door?

Panel 3 (thoughtful)
ART: On the wall, the previous nine art-note reverses form A PLACE TO BE MORE THAN WHAT YOU DO. The final rectangle is empty.
CAPTION: Nine nights had left a sentence waiting.
TREASURY: I still do not have the last word.
DESK BUDDY: Maybe it is not an award.

Panel 4 (clear)
ART: Treasury glances at the blank center of the final note, then at the entrance. Desk Buddy’s old clipboard has COME BACK still unchecked.
CAPTION: Nobody had finished belonging.
TREASURY: Does this note get someone inside?
DESK BUDDY: The door does. Admission is free.

Panel 5 (realizing)
ART: Treasury removes the extra seals. On the note’s reverse, they set the word NEXT in plain, confident type.
CAPTION: The last word made room after itself.
TREASURY: More than what you do next.

Panel 6 (earned)
ART: The complete ten-note wall reads A PLACE TO BE MORE THAN WHAT YOU DO NEXT. The original OPEN FOR WHAT? brass plate hangs below.
CAPTION: The answer was something they had made.
DESK BUDDY: We could have printed that on night one.
TREASURY: We would not have known what it meant.

PAGE 3 — STORY

Panel 1 (warm)
ART: Treasury pulls the final note from the small press. One corner has a slight ink smudge. Desk Buddy points to the clear text and honest edge.
CAPTION: The first proof had been present for the work.
TREASURY: A little smudged.
DESK BUDDY: So is the chair. Keep going.

Panel 2 (welcoming)
ART: Treasury places the notes on a table marked TAKE A SOUVENIR IF YOU LIKE. A separate, larger sign at the entrance reads FREE / COME IN.
CAPTION: The note recorded the night. It did not buy it.
TREASURY: No note required.
DESK BUDDY: Good. I came for the company.

Panel 3 (anticipation)
ART: Desk Buddy hangs the finished invitation at the open door. Treasury turns the marquee from OPEN FOR WHAT? to OPEN FOR EACH OTHER.
CAPTION: At midnight, the ten gathered.
DESK BUDDY: We are ready.
TREASURY: Not finished?

Panel 4 (joyful)
ART: Focus on Treasury and Desk Buddy seated at the edge of the auditorium. The paper cup and clipboard create a shadow cinema on the screen; other occupied seats remain outside the tight frame.
CAPTION: The first program was wonderfully small.
DESK BUDDY: Ready is enough.
TREASURY: Excellent production values on that cup.

Panel 5 (hopeful)
ART: After the show, Treasury sets one extra chair beside the doorway. Desk Buddy places the old clipboard on its seat, COME BACK still open.
CAPTION: One extra seat. One open box.
TREASURY: Who is that for?
DESK BUDDY: Whoever comes next.

Panel 6 (open-ended)
ART: The final art note lies in the empty chair: 10 PLACES on the front, NEXT visible on a folded reverse sample. Through the doorway, dawn meets the warm cinema light.
CAPTION: End of the first ten nights. Beginning of the place.
TREASURY: Shall we do this again?
DESK BUDDY: Put it on the list. Leave the box open.

PAGE 4 — CHARACTER EXTRA
Meet Treasury
Makes money-shaped souvenirs for things that cannot be bought. Learning to honor a moment without making it official.

Favorite detail: the honest edge of a printed object.
Comic weakness: another seal will surely explain the feeling.
New skill: recording significance without assigning a price.

BONUS OBJECT: The ten-note wall sentence: A / PLACE / TO / BE / MORE / THAN / WHAT / YOU / DO / NEXT.
YOUR TURN: Make a souvenir of a small moment. Say what it remembers, not what it is worth.
END CARD: 10 PLACES · Reverse fragment 10/10: NEXT · COLLECTIBLE ART. NO CASH VALUE.
