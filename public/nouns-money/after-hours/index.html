THE STORY SO FAR

They finished the tasks.
Now they need a life.

At midnight, a desktop window becomes a door to Offscreen. Ten useful little companions discover a cinema with forty mismatched chairs, a temperamental projector and a sign that asks Open for what?

Over ten nights, they try to bring it back to life. The checklists help. So do the maps, the memories and the music. But the thing that finally makes the place worth opening is something they cannot tick off a list.

BEYOND THE PANEL

A little Hollywood.
A lot of heart.

A small set, a recognizable cast and a story worth returning to. Three developed entertainment directions carry The Between from paper to screen and into a room with an audience.

01

10 × approximately 2-minute animated shorts

The Midnight Shift

One useful companion trait, one small nighttime problem, one practical visual joke that becomes a real feeling.

Read the treatment

Each short begins on a familiar desktop. Midnight opens a door into Offscreen. A companion attempts to solve an ordinary cinema problem using their daytime specialty: a checklist for belonging, a timetable for spontaneity, a ranking for which door to open. The first minute escalates a readable prop gag. The second gives the character a small relational choice and a warm comic button. The last five seconds reveal an art note from the night. Episodes work alone; the ten reverses form the finale sentence.

02

10 × approximately 11-minute animated episodes, adaptable from the ten comics

The Between

Ten task companions inherit the night shift at a neighborhood cinema, where the hardest job is making room for each other.

Read the treatment

The eleven-minute format adds an A-story and a light B-story while preserving one character spotlight. The A-story follows a practical obstacle to reopening the cinema. The B-story pairs two other companions whose habits create a contrasting joke. These strands meet in a live program, a physical repair or a shared choice. The season has a simple engine—one midnight-to-dawn shift per episode—and a finite first objective: open The Between. After opening, the building becomes an expandable venue for guests, neighborhood mysteries and original genre nights.

03

A 45–60-minute live illustrated comedy and print exhibition

Open for What? A Night at The Between

A miniature cinema is built in front of the audience while its very helpful hosts keep discovering what they forgot to leave room for.

Read the treatment

A host welcomes visitors into an intentionally unfinished cinema. Short projected comic scenes alternate with live paper-folding, an original sound loop and dry prop comedy. Guests may dedicate a card, leave a seat open or make a paper shadow; participation always has a watching-only option. The final sequence combines their voluntary contributions into one temporary wall. The take-home object is an art note or mini-comic, never a promise of financial value. The show ends by turning the empty marquee into OPEN FOR EACH OTHER.

Proposed formats. Animation, talent, festivals and distribution are in development.

A FEW THINGS WE LEARNED FROM THE GREATS

Good ideas
have ancestors.

Selected agency principles across the decades. Our two odes borrow the discipline of economical wit and cinematic spectacle; the words, characters and compositions are our own.

1960s creative revolution

DDB / Bill Bernbach

Make one product truth witty and visually unavoidable.

Here: Plenty of Room uses the small companion as the idea, with new words and its own composition.

DDB source ↗

Late 1960s–1970s theatrical brand worlds

Wells Rich Greene / Mary Wells Lawrence

Turn a brand into a vivid personality people can recognize in a second.

Here: Each Noun gets a recurring ritual, a flaw and an unmistakable graphic setting.

The One Club source ↗

1980s launch spectacle

Chiat/Day

Let one dramatic event make an unfamiliar product emotionally legible.

Here: The Lights Stay On reveals an original night city, with no borrowed plot, prop or slogan.

Andy Hertzfeld / Folklore source ↗

1990s culture and character

Wieden+Kennedy

Create a worldview people can join; build personality beyond product features.

Here: Night Beat and Night Shift Union make belonging visible through fictional crews and rituals.

Nike source ↗

2000s participation as the medium

Droga5

An everyday action can be the campaign itself.

Here: Make a comic panel, fold a keepsake, write a dedication. Participation creates a real artifact.

The One Club source ↗UNICEF source ↗

2010s responsive characters

Wieden+Kennedy

Give a character a repeatable voice and room to respond.

Here: A moderated Reply From the Night Shift format adapts audience prompts into original scenes.

Wieden+Kennedy Tokyo

Build a place for people to make together.
