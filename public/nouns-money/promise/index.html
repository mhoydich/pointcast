The workshop / A promise note

Every note is a promise.

Before money was coins, it was a promise someone was willing to hold. Write one. Keep it, give it away, or tear it up.

A tally stick, a goldsmith's receipt, a bus token, an IOU on a napkin. Each one said the same thing in a different hand: I'm good for it. This page lets you say it on paper.

02 / Understand

What a promise needs to travel.

01 / MAKER

Someone to make it.

A note starts with a person who can actually deliver. Banks, kings and neighbors all began here: with something real behind the paper.

02 / HOLDER

Someone to trust it.

A promise only becomes money when a stranger will take it too. Trust is the part you can't print.

03 / MEMORY

A way to remember it.

Notches, ledgers, serial numbers, blockchains. Every money system is, underneath, a way of not forgetting who owes what.

03 / Possible

Small promises, at scale.

Most of the money in the world is a promise someone else wrote for you. That's fine; it's how strangers trade. But it's worth remembering that the original form still works. You can still make a promise to one person, in your own words, and keep it.

If a hundred neighbors did that, they would have something close to a currency: a shared record of what they've offered and what they've given. That's the question at the center of this workshop. Not what money is, but what it could become when the people holding it also help write it.

Try the neighborhood experiment to watch that happen, or read the histories behind it.

Money has a history. Its future has room for you.
