# Puck · Court Cartographers

## Four rules for deduplicating court listings

1. **Compare the same level of detail.** A park or facility can contain several courts. Link a facility listing to its courts and preserve distinct court numbers even when they share an address or map pin.
2. **Normalize names and addresses to find candidates.** Standardize capitalization, punctuation, and address abbreviations while keeping the original text as an alias. Use nearby coordinates to suggest matches for review, especially when pins mark different entrances.
3. **Confirm court identity before merging.** A matching court ID from the same issuing authority is strong evidence. If IDs are missing, look for corroboration such as a facility map that places both listings on the same numbered court. Keep records separate and flag them for review if court numbers, mapped footprints, or identifiers conflict.
4. **Preserve the evidence after a merge.** Keep one canonical listing and retain source IDs, aliases, and source dates. Carry forward verified details and flag unresolved conflicts in fields such as hours or lighting.

## Two fictional examples

- **Merge.** “Lantern Park Court 2” at 18 Kiln Rd. and “Lantern Pk. #2” at 18 Kiln Road have pins at opposite gates. The same municipal facility map ties both source records to court LP-02. Keep one Court 2 listing with both aliases and source references.
- **Keep separate.** “Copper Yard Court 1” and “Copper Yard Court 2” share an address and the recreation center’s front-door pin. The facility map shows two adjacent, separately numbered playing surfaces. Keep both court listings under Copper Yard.
