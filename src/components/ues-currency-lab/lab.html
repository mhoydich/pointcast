to be kept.

The paper stays the same. Change the story.

What gives your object meaning?

This note has a story and a picture. What is it?

Three different ideas
Collectible
An object kept for its art, story, or interest. This lab creates only an art specimen.
Voucher
A real redemption promise needs an issuer and terms. Gift certificates can fall under consumer rules. Our sample makes no such promise.
Money & financial rights
Money serves exchange, pricing, and saving. Financial rights depend on actual arrangements and applicable law; drawing a note creates none.

What about serials and QR codes?

On U.S. banknotes, serials and plate numbers identify different things. Our label only names a local design. A QR can point to information; its presence alone does not prove ownership or authenticity. Decorative borders can be copied too.

FACILITATOR’S NOTES

A studio in
one sitting.

One screen or one sheet of paper. Pair discussion is optional. Everyone can leave with a private project brief.

01 / 4 MIN

Find the meaning.

Choose an ordinary object worth keeping. Compare reasons without ranking whose memory is more valuable.

Ask: “Valuable to whom?”
02 / 5 MIN

See the hierarchy.

Show two compositions for five seconds each. Have learners name the first thing they saw, then locate the art label.

Ask: “What should lead?”
03 / 4 MIN

Copy the identifier.

Duplicate the note. Compare both labels. Separate a promise to print six from a technical restriction on copying.

Ask: “What did the label prove?”
04 / 7 MIN

Make one proof.

Write the brief, print or draw one note, and test it. Give feedback about what was understood, not whether it looks expensive.

Ask: “What will you revise?”

Bring

Paper, a pencil, and this page. A printer is optional. A plain rectangle with a text description works equally well.

Look for

A clear art label; a reason for the composition; an honest explanation of copying; one observation from testing.

Keep it open

No account, money, wallet, public name, or shared artifact is needed. Read all prompts aloud or use the text path.

AN EL SEGUNDO EXHIBITION IDEA / PROPOSED

The smallest paper museum.

Six art notes. Six stories about a local place. Arrange them on a table with their briefs and ask visitors what they noticed first. Invite a willing host; there is no scheduled event. Keeping your work private counts equally.
