FROM THE RESEARCH SHELF

A visitor changes the room.

Three ideas for an exhibition you can touch.

01 / INVITATION

A form that answers.

David Li’s Blob Opera, made with Google Arts & Culture, turns dragging into an approachable musical interaction. Here, the invitation is sculptural: an orbit, a close look, a change of light. The object should reward curiosity.

02 / LIGHT

Looking is an action.

In Olafur Eliasson’s Your uncertain shadow (growing), visitors interrupt colored light and make changing shadows. In this room, choosing the light offers a simpler question: what does it reveal about a form that was already there?

03 / COMPANY

The show is unfinished.

teamLab’s The Infinite Crystal Universe changes with visitors’ choices and presence. Your three-work exhibition is a smaller, personal expression of that idea: the relationship between objects is another work to make.

Eight families · the material research

All sixty cats are original digital sculptures made for Lucky Cat. The research informs broad questions of form, surface, color, and participation. These are interpretations, with no reproductions or endorsement by the artists, museums, or studios named here.
